The pattern & the fix

Where the landmarks sit now — and where they belong

Same body, two stacks. On the left the ear, ribs and belt-line drift forward of the plumb line; the low back arches to compensate. On the right they stack on the line. The gold arrows are the four corrections you'll drill.

How this works

Two halves, always both

The pattern has two sides, and fixing one without the other doesn't hold. Some things are short and pulling you into the tilt; some things are weak and not holding you out of it. You lengthen the first set and teach the second set to own a neutral pelvis under load — then you rehearse the position until it's your default.

Block 01 · Length

Release what's pulling you forward

Short hip flexors are the main culprit; lats feed the rib-flare and forward head. Length is the smaller half of the job — don't over-invest here.

01

Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch

30–40s × 2 / side
  1. Half-kneeling: one knee down, other foot planted in front, both pointing forward.
  2. Before you move, squeeze the glute of the down leg and tuck your tailbone under (posterior tilt).
  3. Holding that tuck, shift your weight gently forward until you feel a stretch in the front of the down-leg hip/thigh.
  4. Add reach: raise the same-side arm overhead to bias the psoas.
CueThe tuck comes first and stays. A plain lunge-and-lean without it just cranks the low back and misses the flexor.
Lost it whenYou feel it pinching the low back instead of the front of the hip — re-tuck, shorten the lean.
02

Overhead lat stretch

30s × 1–2 / side
  1. Grip a rack, doorframe or shelf overhead; step back and sink hips away.
  2. Exhale and let the ribs settle down — don't arch to reach further.
CueYour overhead reach already looks even and full, so treat this as maintenance, not rescue.
Lost it whenRibs flare forward and the low back arches — that's arching, not lengthening the lat.

Block 02 · Strength & control

Teach a neutral pelvis to hold under load

This is the bigger half. The point isn't "do ab work" — it's owning the stacked position while something tries to pull you out of it.

03

Dead bug (flat-back)

6–8 slow / side × 2–3
  1. On your back, arms reaching at the ceiling, knees stacked over hips at 90°.
  2. Press your low back flat into the floor and keep it there.
  3. Slowly lower the opposite arm and leg toward the floor; return; switch.
CueThe flat back is the exercise. Move only as far as you can go without it lifting.
Lost it whenA gap opens under your low back — you've passed your range. Stop, reset, shorten the reach.
04

Glute bridge

10–12 reps × 2–3, 2s hold
  1. On your back, knees bent, feet flat and close to your hips.
  2. Drive through your heels; squeeze your glutes to lift the hips.
  3. Stop where knee–hip–shoulder make a straight line; hold 2s; lower with control.
CueLift with the glutes, ribs stay down. Don't chase extra height by arching the low back.
Lost it whenYou feel it in the low back, not the glutes — lower the range and re-squeeze.
05

Anti-extension hold — plank or hollow

20–40s × 2–3
  1. Plank on forearms (or hollow-hold on your back), body in one line.
  2. Tuck the ribs toward your belt and gently posterior-tilt the pelvis.
  3. Brace and breathe; the job is to stop the low back arching for the whole hold.
CueRibs to belt, tail slightly tucked, quiet brace. Quality of position beats length of hold.
Lost it whenThe low back sags or arches — drop to knees or shorten the hold to keep the tuck.

Adding load later? Hip thrusts and RDLs done with control train the same neutral-pelvis skill under real weight.

Block 03 · The standing skill

Rehearse the stack until it's your default

This is the piece most people skip, and it's the one that changes what you see in the mirror. No stretch makes you stand well — reps of standing well do.

06

The braced stack

micro-reps, all day
  1. Ribs down toward your belt (kills the flare).
  2. Light ab tension — a quiet brace, not a clench.
  3. Gentle glute engagement to level the pelvis.
  4. Grow tall through the crown; ears drift back over shoulders.
CuePin it to triggers you already hit — kettle boiling, walking through a doorway, stopped at a light. Ten seconds each. Reps beat duration.

Putting it on the week

10–15 minutes, most days

Daily core
Hip flexor stretch · dead bug · glute bridge · one anti-extension hold
All day
Standing-skill micro-reps at your everyday triggers
2–3× / week
Loaded glute work — hip thrust or RDL — if you're already training
Horizon
Judge it over weeks-to-months, not days. Stacks fine on top of current training.

When it gets easy

Progression

Dead bug → slow it further / add end-range holds → light weight in the hands.control first

Glute bridge → single-leg → loaded hip thrust.

Anti-extension hold → longer holds → longer lever (extend the reach) → add a reach or drag.

Standing skill doesn't "progress" — it compounds. The win is when you catch yourself already stacked without cueing it.

If you keep one thing

Rehearse the stack.

Stretching and strengthening set the table, but the thing that changes how you look standing in a mirror is standing well, on purpose, many times a day — until it stops being a pose and becomes the default.

One line worth stating plainly Safety

If this pattern ever produces actual low-back pain — not just the look of it — that's the point to get a hands-on assessment from a physio rather than self-programming. Everything above is for the look and the movement, not for treating pain.

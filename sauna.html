The Sauna Protocol

This is a practice protocol that happens to have health evidence attached, not the other way round. The health case is real but modest and mostly observational — Longevity is right that sauna is garnish, not medicine. What the sauna is unusually good for is reps: the argument to leave shows up reliably, the stakes are low, and a timer can enforce the rule without you.

The rule
The number is set outside the room
"As long as I possibly can" cannot be satisfied, only abandoned. So every session ends in what feels like giving in, and the argument is guaranteed to run.
Decide the minutes before you walk in. Hit it and get out, even if you could have done more — especially then.
It does not move once you are inside. Raising it mid-session is the same failure as lowering it: the room is not where the number gets decided.
Raise it between sessions, not within one. Heat tolerance genuinely adapts, so the number should climb over weeks. That gets you the adaptation without the argument.
This converts an unsatisfiable goal into a completable one, which is the whole intervention. Everything below is what to do while the timer runs.
The two phrases
At the door
The timer decides.
Said before you sit down, while you can still think clearly. If the number is set outside the room and the timer holds it, there is nothing left for you to adjudicate. Your only job is to remain seated.
When it starts
Not relevant. Skin, breath, count.
The same phrase you already use, deliberately — every sauna session is another rep of it. It concedes the thought exists and denies it a vote. The three words after it are where attention goes instead: a sensory channel, not a counter-argument. Arguing back is engagement, and engagement is what keeps it alive.
Deliberately the same phrase as everywhere else in the OS rather than a sauna-specific one. Three or four sessions a week, and the argument shows up reliably in every one — that is roughly thirty extra reps of “not relevant” over eight weeks, against a stable structure that enforces itself.
The line that makes the rest safe
Words are noise. Body is signal. “Not relevant” fires on the first and NEVER on the second.
"This is pointless, you have done enough, you could go now" is commentary — dismiss it.
Dizziness, nausea, a heart rate that will not settle, confusion, or feeling faint on standing are physiology — obey immediately, no deliberation. Leaving on those is not losing the argument.

Muddling those two is the one way this protocol could hurt you. If you are ever unsure which you are hearing, treat it as signal and get out.

This is the scope rule that has always come with the phrase — it is not a general silencer, because some internal signal is load-bearing. Here that is not an abstraction: the load-bearing signal is your physiology, and it outranks the practice every time.
Why the argument shows up at all
You have hit the number many times and nothing bad happened. The arguing continues anyway. That tells you it is not an assessment.
It is not a thought, it is a sensation with words attached. Heat and hunger generate it. More evidence will not settle it, which is why trying to win it is the error.
The argument does not have to stop for you to finish. Both can run at once — the case for leaving, loudly, and you sitting there until the timer goes.
A loud session is a better rep than a quiet one. You are not training a quiet mind. You are training the ability to sit still while it argues. If it never showed up you would get nothing.
The alternative reading — loud session means I am losing ground — is what turns this into another thing to fail at. See Mental Strength on the action crisis: the argument costs you while it runs, and what stops the drain is the question being closed, not which way it closes.
What the evidence actually supports
Frequency and mortality
4–7 sessions a week associated with lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality vs once weekly, with a dose-response. Finnish KIHD cohort, middle-aged Finnish men.		C
Dementia and Alzheimer's
Same cohort, same group, similar dose-response. Weaker than the mortality finding — reverse causation is live, since early cognitive decline reduces sauna-going.		C
Cardiovascular function
Repeated heat exposure improves vascular function and lowers blood pressure. Small randomised trials exist here, not only cohorts. The best-supported claim on the page.		B
Heat shock proteins
Heat reliably induces them. Real biology.		B
HSPs therefore extend human lifespan
Not evidenced. A mechanism is not an outcome, and this leap is where most sauna longevity content goes wrong.		D
Mental toughness transfer
Essentially no direct evidence that heat tolerance transfers to psychological resilience elsewhere. What justifies the practice is the mechanism in the rest of this OS — reps against a stable structure, attention outward, a criterion set in advance — not a sauna literature.		D
The cohort work is observational and the obvious confound is that people who sauna daily are people healthy enough to. Suggestive, not causal. Nothing here is strong enough to justify the practice on health grounds alone — which is fine, because that is not why you are doing it.
Dose
What the cohort used: roughly 19–20 minutes at around 79°C, 4–7 times weekly, traditional Finnish dry sauna. C
What that means for you: your 3–4 sessions a week already sit inside the studied range. Frequency looks to matter more than duration, so more shorter sessions beat fewer heroic ones.
There is no evidence that longer is better. The dose-response in the data is about how often, not how long. That is worth knowing, because the argument you are having is about duration.
Every number in this section is from an observational cohort in a different population. Treat them as a sane range, not a target to optimise. D on any precise figure.
Fasted sessions
Compounding risk
Heat and fasting stack dehydration and drop blood pressure together. On a fasted day the margin is smaller and the body signals arrive sooner and mean more.

Cut the number, do not defend it. A fasted session is not the place to hold a personal best, and the pre-set number should be lower before you walk in. Electrolytes matter more, standing up matters more, and any neurological symptom ends the session immediately.
What this protocol is not
No streak, no log, no tally. The variable already moves — you either hit the number or you did not, and the timer records that. Scoring it would put the auditor back in the room you are clearing it from.
Not a place to test limits. The point is completing a pre-set number calmly, repeatedly. Sessions that go long are not better sessions.
Not medicine. Sleep, aerobic fitness, muscle and food are the high-certainty levers. This sits below all of them on health grounds and above most of them as practice.

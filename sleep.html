The Sleep Protocol

The deep layer under the Longevity protocol's sleep pillar. 2025 research puts insufficient sleep ahead of diet or exercise as a lifespan predictor — and the target is 7–8.5 hours of actual sleep, not time in bed. This is how you get there: anchor the rhythm, then attack onset and depth.

1Fixed RhythmAnchors everything
Same wake time is the single most important behavioural variable — more than duration. It sets when melatonin releases and cortisol peaks.
Fixed wake — 7:00 AM weekdays, within ±30 min on weekends (no later than ~7:30). Sleeping in creates “social jet lag” — the equivalent of flying 1–2 time zones and back every week, which fragments the following week's deep sleep.
Window, not just time in bed — you're aiming for 7–8.5 hours of actual sleep. Onset plus wake-after-onset eats 15–45 min, so a ~10 PM lights-out for a 7 AM wake lands you in range.
Efficiency > length — track total sleep ÷ time in bed on the Amazfit; target >85%. Consistently under 80% means the window is too long — tighten it. A shorter, tight window beats a long restless one (core CBT-I principle).
Guard the Tue/Thu shift nights — those compress you toward ~7 hours. Protect them fiercely; don't also short the morning.
2TemperatureHighest-ROI lever
Your core has to drop ~1–1.5°C to initiate sleep. This is the single most controllable variable in sleep architecture.
Room 18–20°C (65–68°F) — above 24°C fragments deep and REM sleep; even a 3–5°C rise over optimal fragmented architecture in controlled studies. Set the thermostat down by ~21:00.
Warm shower or bath 60–90 min before bed — counterintuitive but well-supported: warming the skin triggers vasodilation, which accelerates the core-temp drop once you get out. For a 10 PM lights-out, that's ~8:30–9:00 PM.
Warm feet, cool room — socks to bed while the room stays cool drives heat dissipation out of the core.
Sauna 4+ hours before bed — great earlier in the day, but too close raises core temperature exactly when you need it falling. Pair it with daytime Zone 2, not the evening.
3LightMaster circadian lever
Light is the most powerful time cue you have. Getting it right sets melatonin release, cortisol timing, and whether the night includes enough deep and REM sleep.
Morning daylight, 5–10 min within 30 min of waking — even cloudy Vancouver light is 5,000–10,000+ lux, far past any indoor bulb. Walking out the door by 07:30 locks it in without adding a step.
Dim after 21:00 — under 50 lux. A Northwestern study found even a dimly lit room (100 lux) during sleep raised heart rate and impaired next-morning glucose versus near-darkness.
Blue and green both matter — blue-blockers alone aren't enough if the room is bright; green light suppresses melatonin too. Dim the room, not just the screen. Screens off after your 20:30 desk offload.
Total darkness at bed — blackout curtains or an eye mask. Even small amounts of light measurably impair architecture.
4Caffeine & AlcoholThe two saboteurs
Both feel harmless at night and both quietly shred sleep quality — caffeine steals depth, alcohol steals REM. Neither shows up as trouble falling asleep, which is why they're missed.
Caffeine hard-stop 14:00 — half-life is 5–7 hours, so a 2 PM cup is still ~half in your system at a 10 PM bed. It selectively cuts deep sleep even when you fall asleep fine; you won't feel it, but the Amazfit deep-sleep number will. If deep sleep runs low, push the cutoff to noon for two weeks and compare.
Alcohol finished 3+ hours before bed — sedation isn't sleep. It helps you fall asleep, then fragments the second half of the night and suppresses REM (where emotional processing, memory consolidation and neural maintenance happen). Even 1–2 drinks within 3 hours cut REM and add awakenings. For a 10 PM bed, last drink by ~7 PM.
Ties to the Longevity protocol's monthly-drinking call — at that frequency this is rarely in play, but on the nights you do drink, the timing is what protects the sleep.
5Training TimingTime the intensity
Exercise improves sleep — but hard training too close to bed spikes sympathetic drive and cortisol that take 2–3 hours to clear.
Hard training (HIIT, heavy strength) — finish 3+ hours before bed (~7 PM for a 10 PM lights-out). Your Wed/Sat HIIT is daytime anyway, so no conflict.
Zone 2 is the gentle exception — because it's genuinely low-strain, it can end as late as ~1.5–2 hours before bed (~8:00–8:30 PM) without harming sleep, and low-intensity work may even shorten sleep onset. The caveat from the largest recent data (4M+ nights): it's strain, not the clock, that disrupts — keep it truly easy (nose-breathing, conversational). If the effort creeps up, fall back to the 3-hour rule.
Morning is still best — training early pairs the session with the morning light hit and anchors the circadian clock. Timing rules are for the days that has to slide later.
6The Pre-Sleep HourPhysiological wind-down
Your evening protocol already handles the cognitive wind-down — desk offload, park the loops, the frame. This is the physical setup in the final 60 minutes.
60 min before — dim to warm-tone bulbs or candles, screens already off, room dropping toward 18–19°C.
30 min before — warm shower now if using the temperature hack; magnesium glycinate 200–400mg supports GABA and muscle relaxation, safe at these doses. Not a melatonin replacement — skip nightly exogenous melatonin without clinical guidance.
At bed — dark, cool, quiet. If the mind is still going, the cognitive shuffle (imagine random unrelated objects in sequence — a dog, a stapler, a mountain, a teacup) occupies the verbal-analytical mind without engaging it. Compatible with your “not relevant” practice — a non-narrative attention anchor.
◎ExtrasSecondary, real
Two more levers that matter less than the six above but are worth knowing.
Napping — keep it under 20 min and before 14:00. Longer enters slow-wave sleep and creates grogginess plus steals night-time sleep drive. Consistently needing naps to function is a signal the night sleep is short — fix the root, don't patch it.
Tracking (Amazfit Active Max) — deep sleep >15% of total, efficiency >85%. Watch the resting-heart-rate trend (rising = overtraining, illness, or poor sleep) and morning HRV via Welltory (a sudden drop confirms a bad night in real time). Judge on 7–14 day rolling averages, never single nights. Don't compare against old Apple Watch data — different algorithms, different baselines.
The priority stack
If you can only do three: fixed wake time → room 18–20°C → morning light within 30 min. Add three more: caffeine off by 14:00 → screens and lights dim by 21:00 → total darkness. Everything else — warm-shower timing, magnesium, nap rules, alcohol timing — is optimization stacked on top of those six.
The one thing
If you change one thing: same wake time every day, 7:00 AM, weekends included. It anchors melatonin, cortisol, and every other lever on this page. Nothing else works as well without it.

The five laws

Law one

Wait on quarterback, then take one who runs

You can rent a quarterback. You cannot rent a starting back.

  • One QB, twelve teams — at least twenty are startable. The position is deepest relative to your need.
  • Rushing yards score at 10 per point against 25 passing; rushing scores are 6 against 4. His legs are worth 2.5× his arm.
  • 500 rushing yards and five scores is roughly 80 points of free floor — near five a game a pocket passer can't match.
  • Push QB into the back third. Target the ~52–73 band, then carry a late runner as a streamer.
Law two

Don't force Zero-RB — secure two backs, then chase value

Half-PPR pays receivers without letting them run away with it.

  • No gimmick build required. Running back is the scarcer position and the pool thins fast.
  • Lock two backs you trust in the first handful of rounds.
  • After that, best player available. Let the flex sort itself out.
Law three

Decide your tight end early — pay up or punt, never dabble

The seventh-best tight end costs you the pick and buys you no edge.

  • Real weekly advantage exists in the top two. Below them it's a swamp.
  • Buy the edge, or skip it entirely and stream off your free waiver economy.
  • Pick the lane before draft night. Do not improvise it live.
Law four

Kicker and defense in the last two rounds — one each

Both are matchup plays and both are free to churn every week.

  • Never two of either. Never before your final two picks.
  • Draft the Week 1 matchup, not the best unit on paper.
  • Every earlier pick spent here is a bench stash or an upside dart you gave away.
Law five

The draft is half the game — the wire is the other half

Your Week 10 roster should barely resemble your draft.

  • No-cap rolling waivers mean the manager who works the wire beats the manager with the better draft.
  • Build for it: draft upside on the bench, not safe veterans you'll never start.

Positional playbook

Quarterback

Let it come to you, then take the legs

  • Premium legs: Jayden Daniels, Jalen Hurts, Lamar Jackson if he slides.
  • Cheaper runners: Caleb Williams, Bo Nix, Jaxson Dart. Kyler Murray is genuine value in a better offense.
  • Keep a second streamer churning early in the year until one hits.
  • No non-rushing quarterback early, however good the name.
Running back

Two anchors, then darts

  • You start two plus a flex — two you'll start every week and one or two high-upside darts, not a stable.
  • Two IR slots let you draft an injured back who will return without costing a bench spot.
  • Handcuff your own starter. The rolling-waiver crowd will race you for his backup the moment he goes down.
  • In flux: Brooks has passed Hubbard in Carolina; Braelon Allen gains with Hall banged up; MarShawn Lloyd is the Jacobs dart; Skattebo healthy and sharing; Irving full-go.
  • Let someone else reach for Pacheco while that knee heals.
Wide receiver

Volume over highlight

  • Half-PPR pays target hogs. Nine looks a game beats the man who needs a long touchdown to pay off.
  • The 40-yard bonus is a tiebreaker toward field-stretchers, not a primary filter.
  • Past their draft position: Malik Nabers (trending to play Week 1, still underpriced), Josh Downs, Michael Wilson, Chris Olave.
  • Jordyn Tyson: very late stash only. Alec Pierce, on PUP with an ankle: skip.
Tight end

Two names or none

  • Paying up: Brock Bowers or Trey McBride. Take one and forget the position all year.
  • Punting: the middle pivots are Tyler Warren and Tucker Kraft. Past them it's stream-and-pray, which is fine given free adds.
Kicker & defense

Rentals, not assets

  • Week 1 matchup first: domes and good offenses for the kicker, a soft opponent for the defense.
  • Ten points for a shutout plus sacks, takeaways and yardage makes a great weekly matchup worth more than a big name in a bad spot.

Draft-slot branches

Picks 1–4

The anchor

  • You get a true anchor — an elite back or one of the top two receivers.
  • More than twenty picks before you're back, so a run will break out. Plan for it.
  • Watch which tier is about to empty and be willing to reach a touch for need at the turn.
  • Elite back plus top-tier receiver across your first two is the cleanest early build.
Picks 5–8

The forgiving middle

  • You catch the second wave of elite talent and you're never far from your next pick.
  • Pure best-player-available. No reaching required.
  • Just don't chase a position before its tier breaks.
Picks 9–12

Win on the doubles

  • Back-to-back picks at every turn — a gift for grabbing two players out of a cliffing tier.
  • First turn: pair two premium players, or two backs if the run is on.
  • Pre-empt runs. If quarterbacks or the top tight ends start flying, take two before the next turn.
  • Your single best pick is a notch lower. Accept it and win on the doubles.

Draft-night sequence

  1. Morning of the 30th. Re-pull risers, fallers and the injury report. Confirm nobody in your target tiers got hurt or lost a job overnight.
  2. Rebuild the Yahoo queue so it matches the refreshed tiers.
  3. Name your three cliffs — usually elite tight end, your running-back floor, and the dual-threat quarterback run.
  4. 8:10 PM, the order drops. Read your slot and jump to the matching branch.
  5. Pre-decide your first two picks across the two or three likeliest scenarios. The clock is one minute — spend it reacting, not deciding.
  6. Early rounds. Two starting backs and an anchor receiver before the middle third.
  7. Middle rounds. Dual-threat quarterback, the honoured tight-end call, upside receivers with real target volume.
  8. Late rounds. Handcuff your own back, a couple of lottery tickets, a streaming quarterback, then kicker and defense last.

The waiver edge

Where leagues are won

There are two ways to add a player and only one of them costs you anything.

  • A successful claim drops you to the bottom of the priority order.
  • Dropped players sit on waivers two days, processing Tuesday at game time. Once they clear they're free agents — no priority spent.
  • Spend priority only on genuinely contested must-haves: the emerging starter after an injury, the back who just inherited a backfield.
  • Everything else — bye fill-ins, streaming defenses, a second kicker, a matchup quarterback — grab free once waivers clear.
  • No acquisition cap means churn the back of your bench weekly. Your only scarce resource is the priority slot.
  • The wire opens the morning after Week 1. Claims in before the Tuesday deadline, every week, all year.

In-season management

Weeks 1–17
  • Start matchups, not names. By midseason your streamers will sometimes outscore your draft picks.
  • Flex for ceiling. Highest volume or best matchup regardless of position. Don't default to a running back out of habit.
  • Work both IR slots continuously. Free real estate — never sit on an empty slot while a startable free agent goes unclaimed.
  • Trade before Nov 28, and package deals so both sides visibly get something, because trades go to a league vote.
  • Trade toward Weeks 15–17. A slightly worse player with a great playoff schedule beats a better one facing top defenses.
  • Play for a top-two seed. Six of twelve make it, reseeding is on, and the top two get a Week 15 bye. Don't coast late. Confirm the exact bracket in your settings.

The one thing

Draft by tiers breaking, not by ranks. Every time you're on the clock, glance at which tier is one player from emptying — that cliff, not the rank list, tells you what to take.

# PARAMOUNT PROTOCOL — reference card

The practice that lives in the morning brief (**Paramount Protocol** card) and is
reviewed each night (**Protocol Review**, step 7 of the evening debrief). This is the
plain-language index of what each item means and how it's counted. Derived from the
Terrain Doctrine; kept here so you don't have to reopen the full doctrine to remember
what a line is asking for.

---

## The five terrains

Everything traces back to the one Highland idea worth keeping: **you win by choosing
where and when the fight happens, not by fighting harder.** Douglas never defended what
he couldn't hold; Mac Colla chose ground where the enemy's advantage couldn't cycle.

---

## TERRAIN — choose the ground, refuse what you cannot hold

*Header image: Highland warriors.*

- **Boundary Rep** — State one preference, or decline one small thing. No qualifier, no
  apology. Let the silence sit. *Counted: once per instance.*
- **Warrior Promise** — Name the one hard thing you're avoiding, do it first, before the
  easy stuff. *Counted: once per instance (usually once a day).*
- **Opportunity Scanning** — On any setback or new environment: *"What opportunity does
  this situation present me with?"* *Counted: once per instance.*

Minutes field: total time spent deliberately practising terrain moves today.

---

## TEMPO — nothing hurried, nothing anxious

*Header image: John Wayne.*

- **Pause before moving, deciding or acting** — the one you're building. When you catch
  yourself drawing breath to speak, let it out *without words*, shoulders down, then
  speak. Two seconds, identical every time. Counted in four separate places so you can
  see where it lands and where it doesn't:
  - **speaking** — the breath before the word
  - **reaching** — the hand extending toward phone, door, keys
  - **deciding** — the moment you notice you're deciding (expect this one low; you
    usually notice you *have* decided — that's data, not failure)
  - **getting up** — before you stand or move
- **Slow Movements & Speech** — Move and speak ~20% slower than your impulse. Reach,
  turn, walk slower. *Counted: once per instance you catch it.*
- **I'm Fine Either Way** — Before the first tense interaction, say it silently and mean
  it. You'd like it to go well; you don't need it to. *Counted: once per instance.*

Minutes field: total time spent deliberately practising tempo today.

Target for the pause: **three a day per cue to start.** Not all day — three. Raise it
only when three has become boring, never on a schedule.

## PRESENCE — no performance in this one

*Header image: Musashi.*

- **Not Relevant** — When the optimizing narrative starts (how to improve this, what it
  should become), name it and take away its authority: *not relevant to being present
  with my family.* It can stay. It doesn't get a vote. Scope: optimization thoughts only,
  never a general silencer — some internal signal is load-bearing.

**Deliberately uncounted.** No tally, no score, no tick. A presence practice that comes
with a way to fail stops being one. The debrief gives it a single optional note field and
nothing else. If you ever find yourself wanting to count it, that impulse is the exact
thing this section exists to catch.

---

## CAL AI — standalone

- **Cal AI before meals** — Log nutrition before eating. Every meal. *Counted: meals
  logged today.*

---

## How the counting works

- The morning card is **reference only** — no buttons. You read it, you don't tap it.
- The evening debrief's **Protocol Review** step is where the numbers are entered.
- Counts are stored per-date under `forge-protocol-counts`, synced phone↔Mac, and folded
  into the daily log and CSV export — so a future "pause reps vs day-feel" or "Cal AI
  adherence vs nutrition" analysis has the data.
- Presence is never in the count. By design.

This is a **reference document, not a brief component.** The brief is generated HTML —

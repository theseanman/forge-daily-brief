Conceptual Spiral

After Col. John Boyd, The Conceptual Spiral (1992), from A Discourse on Winning and Losing. How new understanding gets made. Shaping and Adapting fixes a picture that has drifted; this one is about generating the next picture at all.

Change
The idea
The world keeps unfoldingDOCTRINE
Boyd’s claim is that novelty is not an occasional event but the permanent condition. The world keeps producing situations nobody has seen, which means every picture you hold decays whether or not anyone attacks it, and whether or not you are doing anything wrong.
The consequence is uncomfortable: there is no state of being finished. A method that worked is not evidence that it will keep working — only that the world hasn’t moved yet. Staying matched isn’t maintenance, it’s continuous production.
The engine is a loop of pulling things apart and putting them back together differently, run over and over. Each turn widens what you can see, which is why Boyd drew it as a spiral rather than a circle: you don’t return to where you started, you come back round at a wider radius.
A circle returns you to where you started. A spiral doesn’t.
Where this sits next to Shaping and Adapting. That page is corrective — it catches a picture that has drifted from reality and fixes it. This one is generative: it is about producing the next idea rather than repairing the current one. They touch at exactly one point, its import frames from outside your domain move, which is the same instinct read from the other side. Everything else here is new ground.
The five moves
Produce, don’t consumeB
A conclusion you read is someone else’s synthesis. Nothing of yours has been tested, so nothing has spiralled.
Use when you’ve just finished a book, a paper, a protocol doc or a walk of listening and feel like you’ve learned something.
Ask what you now do differently. If the answer is nothing, you’ve consumed. That isn’t worthless — it’s raw material — but it is not the thing itself.
Convert one idea into something testable before the next input. One claim, one prediction, one attempt. Generating your own version of material beats re-reading it, by a wide and well-replicated margin. B
Notes are not the conversion. A note records someone else’s thought at a slightly lower fidelity. The conversion is an attempt that could fail.
In practice
The walksSpeechify listening is intake. End the walk with one thing to try this week, not a note.Ten protocols listened to and none run is ten inputs and nought turns of the spiral.
Self-EvidenceThis is the method the course sells, aimed at you.Your own reading before the master’s was no comparison to action research — that difference is exactly this move.
Room 148A strategy you read about becomes real when one class has run it and you’ve seen the work.Until then it’s a strategy you can describe, which is a different thing from one you can use.
Failure signal: your input rate is high and your attempt rate is flat. Reading feels like progress because it produces the sensation of understanding without the risk of being wrong.
Name it: refining or generatingC
Improving inside the current frame and building a new frame feel identical from the inside. Only one of them widens anything.
Use when you sit down to work on something that already exists.
Say which one this session is. Refining the worksheet generator is not the same work as asking whether worksheets are the right vehicle.
Both are legitimate — the danger is drift. Refinement is comfortable, endless, and always available, so it becomes the default without a decision ever being made.
Check the ratio, not the session. A month of pure refinement is a signal. A single session of it is just a session.
In practice
Forge OSAnother card, another installer, another page — almost always refinement.The generating question is whether the system is changing what you do, which no new card answers.
IncomePolishing the course is refinement; putting it in front of someone who might pay is generating.The second produces information the first cannot.
Stock systemTuning parameters is refinement inside the frame. Asking what the model assumes about the market is generating.Frames expire quietly; parameters tuned inside an expired frame get better at the wrong thing.
Failure signal: every recent session was refinement, and each was individually justified. They usually are.
Many small turns, not one leapDOCTRINE
Novelty comes from the number of cycles, not the size of any one of them. It is a spiral, and spirals are made of turns.
Use when you’re waiting to have a good enough idea before starting.
Shrink the cycle until it fits the time you actually have. Two hours a week is enough for a turn. It is not enough for a leap, and waiting for one means no turns at all.
Let the turns be unimpressive. A small test that returns a real result beats a large one that never runs.
Count turns, not hours. Hours reward sitting with it; turns reward closing it.
In practice
The two hoursDuring term, design the week’s work as one complete turn rather than a slice of a big build.A slice produces nothing to learn from until the whole thing lands, which may be July.
Room 148One activity changed in one block is a turn, and you get the result Monday.A whole unit rebuilt over the break is one turn for the entire summer.
The booksA chapter section put in front of one reader is a turn.A finished manuscript nobody has read is a single enormous bet on a frame you set months ago.
Failure signal: you can’t remember the last time something you made met a real reaction. Long gaps between turns mean the frame has been running untested.
Close the turn against realityA
A turn ends when something outside you responds. Thinking it through to a satisfying conclusion is not the end of a cycle.
Use when an idea feels finished.
Write what you expect before you look. A written when–then makes the result informative instead of reinterpretable, and roughly doubles follow-through on the attempt itself. A
Put it somewhere it can fail. One class, one reader, one week of live data. An idea that cannot be wrong cannot teach you anything.
Read the miss, not the score. The direction and size of the error is the whole payload. A hit tells you much less.
In practice
Room 148Before the activity, write what you expect to see in the work.Then the exit tickets are a test rather than a mood reading.
Stock systemThe forward test already is this. The discipline is writing the expectation first.Without it, any outcome can be explained after the fact, and usually is.
IncomeName what a successful response looks like before you send anything.“Two replies asking about price” is a test. “See how it goes” is not.
Failure signal: the prediction gets written after the result is known, or it was vague enough to survive either outcome.
Give the frame an expiryC
Decide in advance when you will rebuild, rather than waiting for the frame to fail loudly enough to force it.
Use when something is working well. That is the moment, not when it breaks.
Put a date on it. “I rebuild this in January” beats “I rebuild it when it stops working,” because a decaying frame does not announce itself.
Expect the new version to start worse. A rebuild that begins below the old version is the ordinary cost of the method, not evidence it failed.
Keep what worked, deliberately. Carry the pieces forward. Boyd’s point was never to throw things away, it was to break them apart so they can be recombined.
In practice
CoursesA unit that has run three years unchanged is due, regardless of how well it goes.Familiar and effective are different properties that feel the same from the front of a room.
Forge OSThe quarterly triage is the existing expiry mechanism. It killed nothing on its first pass.An expiry you never enforce is a wish.
HealthA training block that has stopped producing change has expired even if you still like it.Adaptation is the measure, not adherence.
Failure signal: nothing has been rebuilt in a year, and everything feels fine. That combination is the ordinary appearance of a frame quietly going stale.

The one thing
What did I make this week that could have been wrong? If nothing, the spiral didn’t turn, however much went in.
Failure modes
Consumption dressed as method. More books, more protocols, more listening — all of it feels like the spiral and none of it turns it. This is the main risk of this page and of the walks.
Novelty for its own sake. The point is staying matched to a moving world, not being interesting. A new frame that nothing tested is just a different untested frame.
Turning this into a build. Nothing here needs a tracker. A tracker for spiral turns would be refinement of the OS, which is precisely the failure mode move two names.
Abandoning too early. Give the frame an expiry — a date — rather than rebuilding whenever the current thing gets hard. Both extremes stop the spiral.
Counting hours. Hours in the chair are input. Closed turns are the output, and they are the only number worth watching here.
Evidence
DOCTRINE — Boyd’s own argument, built from his reading of science, engineering and history. Coherent and influential, but interpretation rather than an empirical finding. No study sits behind it and none is claimed.
A — strong, replicated experimental support. Used here for implementation intentions and written predictions (Gollwitzer).
B — good supporting research, narrower in scope: the generation effect and testing effect — producing and retrieving material yourself beats re-reading it, robustly, across many studies (Slamecka & Graf; Roediger & Karpicke).
C — reasoned from your own records and this system’s history rather than from research. The refinement-drift and expiry moves are in this class.
D — weak or contested. Nothing on this page is graded D.
Boyd delivered The Conceptual Spiral in 1992, late in the sequence, and it is the most abstract of the briefings. He never published any of it; the slides are terse because he talked over them for hours.

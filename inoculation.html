Inoculation

Solo, cheap, injury-free drills that raise the load you can still think under. This page owns the body under stress and the pre-decided first move. In-the-moment composure is Resilience; the willpower tracks are Daily Challenge; the physical skills are Self-Defense; the social-exposure ladder is the Fear of Failure doc. None of those is restated here.

Change
Why this exists. Watching danger from a seat builds a model of the threat and nothing else. Forty documentaries on predators make a well-informed victim. Repeated exposure to violent media does lower your heart rate at the next clip B, and that is a dulled alarm, not a better response: no study finds it improves what you do when the thing is real. Readiness is built the other way round, and it is cheap.
The one rule
Arousal → task → feedback
Anything that only produces the arousal is a cold shower. Anything that adds a task and a score is inoculation.
Stress inoculation training B and the stress-exposure training used in police, military and aviation B share one finding: performance holds under pressure only when the practice included the pressure, and the practice was active, with a decision made inside the stress and feedback on it afterward. Mental practice helps B, but only first-person rehearsal of you doing the thing, and it works best layered on the physical rep.
Every drill below has three parts. A cheap way to raise arousal. A task with a score. A number written down before and after.
Write the prediction first. The learning comes from the gap between what you predicted and what happened B. A rep without a prediction is a rep without the active ingredient.
The seven drills
01
Cold with a job
A cold shower or a cold bath, and while you are in it, a task you can score.
SETUP Two to three minutes of cold water. Before you turn it on, write the predicted intensity (1–10) and how long until it feels manageable.
The task: count backward from 300 by sevens aloud, or recite the thing you are memorizing. Score it: how far you got, how many errors.
What it buys: repeated cold reliably blunts the body’s alarm to cold B, with weaker evidence that the calm carries to other stressors C. The arithmetic is what turns habituation into training: a clear head in the first ten seconds of any shock.
Log: predicted vs actual intensity, task score, peak heart rate if the watch catches it.
Safety: shower or tub only. No open water, no face-first plunges alone. Cold shock is the one real risk and the bathtub removes it.
02
Air hunger, on dry land
Breath holds on the couch, with a decision task running during the hold.
SETUP Sitting. Normal exhale, then hold. Predict how many seconds until the urge becomes loud, and how you will rate it.
The task: sort a shuffled deck by suit, or solve three mental-arithmetic problems, during the hold. Score accuracy and how far you got.
What it buys: CO₂-tolerance work reduces panic-type reactivity to the body’s own alarm signals C. The point is making a clean choice while the body is yelling.
Log: predicted vs actual intensity, hold length, task accuracy.
Safety: never in water, never standing, never after hyperventilating. Stop at the first strong urge, not past it.
03
Precision after intensity
Thirty seconds of burpees or stairs to a high heart rate, then immediately a fine-motor or recall task.
SETUP Watch on. Predict the heart rate you will reach and whether the task will hold.
The task: thread a needle, dial a specific ten-digit number from memory, write one legible sentence, or lay out a four-step plan aloud. Score it 0–10.
What it buys: this is the home version of stress shooting B in the field, C for the home version. It raises the heart rate at which your hands and your working memory still function, and it gives you the hard number: the heart rate at which the task falls apart, and whether that number climbs over weeks.
Log: peak heart rate, task score, predicted vs actual. This drill feeds the Load chart.
Safety: it is thirty seconds, not a workout. Stop for chest pain, dizziness, or anything that is not ordinary breathlessness.
04
The random alarm
An alarm at an unknown time once a day. When it fires, sixty seconds to execute a pre-decided first move for one scenario.
SETUP Set it the night before for a time you will not remember. Rotate the four scenarios below. Predict how long the first move will take and whether you will hesitate.
The task: the first move only, timed. Not the whole response, the first move, because the freeze lives in the first move.
What it buys: a pre-decided plan fires where an undecided one freezes. Implementation intentions carry a medium-to-large effect on follow-through A; the evidence that drills specifically improve emergency response is thinner C. The widely repeated claim that 80% of people freeze in a disaster is poorly sourced D, but the freeze itself is real and the countermeasure is the move that is already chosen.
Log: seconds to first move, hesitation yes or no, which scenario.
05
Time pressure on camera
The Trier Social Stress Test, solo: a five-minute timed speech on a topic you know badly, camera running, then watch it back.
SETUP Phone propped, timer visible, topic drawn at random. Predict how bad it will be (1–10) and how it will feel to watch.
The task: speak the full five minutes. No stopping, no restarting. Then watch it and score it 0–10 against the prediction.
What it buys: the Trier test is one of the most reliable stress inducers in the lab B, and the recording is what makes the solo version real. What you are training is the accuracy of your forecasts of social cost: predicted versus actual, again and again, until the forecast stops being wrong in the same direction B. This overlaps rungs four and six of the Fear of Failure ladder; a rep here counts there.
Log: predicted vs actual, score on playback, how long the discomfort lasted.
06
Heat and hunger with a task
You already sauna and you already fast. Each is a real stress state. The inoculation is doing judgment work inside it.
SETUP Pick a task that needs judgment and has a visible quality: marking a set, making a decision you have been avoiding, drafting a message that matters. Predict whether the quality will hold.
The task: do it in the state. Score the quality afterward, when you are out of it, against the prediction.
What it buys: both are general stressors C; the task pairing is reasoning, not a finding. What you get is a log of whether your judgment degrades under physical load, and by how much. For most people it degrades less than they predict, which is the lesson.
Log: predicted vs actual, task quality score.
Safety: the sauna and fasting rules you already run. Nothing is added to the dose; only the task is.
07
First-person imagery
Five minutes, eyes closed, one scenario run as yourself in detail, including the fear, and the first move performed.
SETUP Pick the scenario from the four below, or the next real situation you are avoiding. Predict how vivid you can make it and whether you will flinch away from the hard part.
The task: stay in it for the full five minutes. Every time the mind leaves, bring it back to the body in the scene. Score how many times it left.
What it buys: mental practice carries a moderate effect on performance B, strongest when layered on the physical drill, weakest on its own. It installs the script so that the random alarm has something to fire.
Log: predicted vs actual vividness, number of exits, scenario.
The four scenarios, and their first moves
The alarm and the imagery rotate through these. A first move is one action, decided now, that you can do in under ten seconds. It is not the plan. It is the thing that breaks the freeze so the plan can start.
A
Violence
A confrontation, or someone in the house who should not be.
First move: distance and voice. Two steps back, hands up open, one loud clear sentence. In the house: the door between you and them, then the phone.
Drill it: at the alarm, stand, take the two steps, say the sentence out loud. Time it. The technique after that is Self-Defense’s page, not this one.
B
Medical
Someone collapses, chokes, or bleeds in front of you.
First move: say “911” aloud and point at a person, or dial it yourself if alone. Then hands on: back blows for choking, pressure for bleeding, chest compressions for no pulse.
Drill it: at the alarm, say your address and the dispatcher script aloud without calling, then thirty seconds of compressions on a cushion at the right tempo. A four-hour first-aid course is the one spend on this page worth money.
C
Fire or crash
Smoke in the townhouse, or the car has stopped the wrong way.
First move, fire: low, to the nearest exit, door closed behind you, meet point. First move, crash: hazards on, engine off, check yourself before you move, then out and away from the road.
Drill it: at the alarm, walk the exit with the lights off, or find the extinguisher and pull the pin on an imaginary fire. Once a month, the whole family walks it with you.
D
Public exposure
The hard question from a parent, the student who catches your error in front of the class, the room going quiet on you.
First move: one breath out, then say the true thing first. “You’re right, I got that wrong.” “I don’t know; I’ll find out.” The sentence is pre-written so it does not have to be composed under load.
Drill it: at the alarm, say one of the three sentences aloud, full voice, as if to the room. This is the scenario the camera drill trains most directly.
The gate
Hold a drill until the forecast closes
No fixed rungs. One gate per drill, read off the log.
A drill is banked when two consecutive reps show a predicted-versus-actual gap of one point or less, no safety behaviour, and a task score of six or better. That is the forecast telling you it has stopped being wrong.
A banked drill drops to boosters: one rep every two weeks. Return of fear is predicted by the evidence A in animals, B in people: the original alarm is inhibited, not erased. The booster is what keeps the new rule on top.
Cadence overall: two or three reps a week across the drills, most under ten minutes. Two weeks off and a banked drill goes back into training.

The honest limit. Transfer is narrow. You get calmer and more functional in situations that resemble the drills, and the evidence for far transfer, a general courage that shows up anywhere, is weak. So this page does not promise bravery. It promises three specific things: a smaller gap between what you predict and what happens, a higher load at which you still function, and a first move that is already chosen. Those three are what the brave-looking person actually has.
Cashing it in
Spend it within a week
Capacity you do not use decays the way fear returns. Every banked drill should produce a real-world spend inside seven days, and the spend gets logged.
1. The ask for money. One real person, one real price for Self-Evidence. The predicted cost of the no was the whole blocker; the camera drill is what makes that forecast accurate. This is where the capacity pays most.
2. The ten coach emails. Same mechanism, at scale. Count replies; silence is data.
3. The hard conversation you have been managing around. A parent, a student, a colleague. First move D, then the plan.
4. Teaching under observation. Invite the colleague in. The load is real and the prediction is checkable.
5. Krav, when the rung arrives. The highest-load legal simulation of violence; the precision drill is its warm-up.
6. The real emergency. You do not schedule it. The first move already exists, and the log says you have executed it under load. That is the whole return on the page.
Log every spend in the Log tab as a Spend entry. The chart then shows the return on the drills, not just the drills.
Synthesis
The hard number
Every protocol on this list that has moved you had a number. This one’s is the heart rate at which fine-motor skill collapses, from drill 03, measured by what is already on your wrist. It is the readiness equivalent of a sleep score: a wish becomes a figure, and a figure can climb.
Predict before you act
The prediction field on every rep is Shaping and Adapting’s third move run on the body. The exposure research says the same thing from the other side: the learning is in the violation, and there is no violation without a prediction.
Home ground first
Your own call on the Second Nature ladder: start where self-monitoring is lowest. Drills 01, 02, 06 and 07 are at home with no one watching. Drill 03 is the first one with a number anyone could see. The camera is the first one with an audience, even if it is only you later.
The cue
One line for the corner, in the shape Cus’s Corner uses: “Task, not feeling.” Said at the moment the arousal arrives, it points at the only thing you can control inside it.
Why the spectator version feels like progress
Humans are drawn to simulated encounters with threats, at a distance that keeps the arousal in the enjoyable band, and they come away with a model of the threat B for the general account, C for true crime and combat sport specifically. The model is real. The capacity is not. Boxing on a screen gives you the arousal and withholds the rep; this page gives you the rep and lets the screen keep the arousal.
The one thing
Arousal is free. The rep is the task you do inside it. Anything that raises your heart rate is a drill the moment you add a score and a prediction. Nothing that raises your heart rate is a drill without them.
Failure modes
Arousal with no task. Cold showers for their own sake, sauna as relaxation, breath holds as a party trick. Pleasant, and not this protocol.
No prediction. A rep without the number written first produces habituation at best. It is the cheapest step and the one most often skipped.
Training the easy drills only. Cold and imagery can be repeated indefinitely because nobody sees them. If three weeks in there is no camera rep and no spend, that is the data.
Dosing up instead of banking. Colder water and longer holds are a different hobby. The gate is forecast accuracy, not tolerance; once a drill is banked, the next step is spending it, not escalating it.
Sleep deprivation as a stressor. Out. It impairs the learning you are trying to bank. So is anything with impact or falls done alone.
Banked and unspent. A drill banked with no spend inside seven days has produced a feeling of readiness and nothing else.
Evidence key
A strong — replicated across many studies or meta-analyses · B good — several independent studies, some mixed · C suggestive — few studies or small effects · D thin — one study or theory only. Anywhere a claim is extended to you specifically, it is reasoning, and the log is the only evidence that will ever be about you.

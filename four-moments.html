Which moment is this?

Two questions. Run it at the boundary — before you walk in, before you sit down, before you start the drive. Not once you are already in the thing. If you genuinely cannot tell mid-moment, the default is attention outward and sort it afterwards.

The Three Moments Protocol

Every moment is one of three kinds, and each asks for a different sort of thinking. The hard line is the moment, not the thought — judging each thought on its merits is impossible in real time and is itself a form of monitoring.

Two things do not get to run here. The loop — circling why am I like this. And the replay — rehearsing a conversation that already happened. Both feel like thinking and produce nothing, and an unoccupied mind is exactly where they thrive.

Neither the loop nor the replay feels like an optimising thought, so "not relevant" may not catch them on its own. Same disposal though — name it, one channel, back to the walk.

Going into and leaving work is not a moment — it is the cue. That is where the gate fires, and they are the two most valuable thresholds you have.

Scrolling does not fit, and that is not a gap in the model. It occupies attention without demanding it, which makes it the one activity that prevents drifting rather than permitting it. It competes with Free and usually wins.

Intervals move cardio to Task. A struggling student moves teaching to Someone.

One thing the table hides. The chair and the drive are both Free, but only the chair is unhurried enough for the big unresolved questions — direction, the exit, what kind of father you are being. Those need somewhere to go. If no stretch of your day is unhurried, that thinking does not disappear; it leaks into the other two.
Inside Task: the four tests
2 is the one to memorise. Concrete versus abstract is the split that predicts outcomes — not positive versus negative, not planning versus worrying. "Email him Tuesday with the two options" and "how should I be handling this whole situation" are the same topic in two modes, and only one of them ends.
How this retires
Every use adds to the table. Sort a new situation once and it stops being a question. The tool consumes its own reason to exist.
Fade it deliberately. Weeks one to three, run all three questions. Then predict the answer first and only check yourself. Then stop checking.
The new stuff is the point. Sixteen situations are already decided. Richmond High in September will produce a month of unfamiliar ones, and that is what this is for.
If you are still running it daily at week eight, that is information rather than a failure — it means the discrimination has not formed yet, and the honest response is more reps, not more tools.
What this does not cover
Emotion is not a thought to be triaged. Grief, dread, the thing that surfaces at 2am — these fail every test and the correct response is not to name them and return to one channel. Sometimes the work is the sitting with it.
People are not tasks. Asking what action a thought about someone would change is instrumental, and people can feel it.
Unfamiliar ground needs slow, abstract thinking. Test 2 penalises why questions, but why is the right question when there is no pattern yet to recognise.
This is a framework for attention, not for processing. Open time is a slot, not a method. If something keeps arriving that time alone does not resolve, that is outside what any flowchart does well.
Evidence
A Strong. Kahneman & Klein (2009) on when intuition can be trusted — an adversarial collaboration. Automaticity as instance retrieval rather than faster processing (Logan 1988).

B Good. Watkins on concrete versus abstract repetitive thought — the split that predicts outcomes, not positive versus negative. Wood on context cues carrying habits. Gollwitzer on implementation intentions. Beilock & Carr on monitoring degrading skilled execution.

C Suggestive. Incubation and the creative value of mind-wandering. Real effects, smaller and less consistent than the popular framing. Flow research generally is self-report and correlational — treat it as vocabulary, not evidence.

D Arbitrary. Every threshold and interval here, including the three-week fade. No evidence sets those numbers; they are fixed in advance so they cannot be renegotiated later.

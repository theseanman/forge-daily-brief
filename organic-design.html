Organic Design for Command and Control

After Col. John Boyd, Organic Design for Command and Control (1987), from A Discourse on Winning and Losing. Why watching more closely gets you worse information, and what to do instead.

The idea
Command and control is the wrong frameDOCTRINE
Boyd’s argument is that the words themselves cause the damage. Command implies directing people who are further from the situation than they are, and control implies that a central point can hold an accurate picture of what everyone is doing. Neither survives contact with anything fast-moving.
His replacement pair is appreciation and leadership. Appreciation is getting a true picture from the people who actually have it. Leadership is giving them enough of the intent that they can act without asking. Neither requires you to know what everyone is doing, which is fortunate, because you can’t.
The mechanism underneath is that a centralised node is a bottleneck on tempo. Every decision routed through one person adds that person’s cycle time to everyone else’s.
The left picture is slower, and it is also where the information is worst.
Where this sits next to the other pages. Patterns of Conflict’s seventh move, intent not instructions, is the one-line version of this and stays there. This page owns the two halves that move doesn’t touch: what monitoring does to the information you get, and the receiving side — how your reaction to a report decides what you are told next. Second Nature is about your own automaticity; this is about everyone else’s. Nothing here is adversarial, so it is safe in every room you are in.
The five moves
The monitoring paradoxB
Watching a thing changes what gets reported about it. Past a point, closer monitoring buys you worse information, not better.
Use when you want more visibility into how something is going, and your instinct is to add a check, a report or a tracker.
Assume the measure will be optimised, not the outcome. Any number used to judge people stops measuring what it measured. This is one of the better-supported effects in organisational research. B
Ask what the check costs the person being checked. Time, yes, but mostly it tells them which parts of their work you are watching, and that is where their effort will go.
Prefer the artefact to the report. Look at the work itself, which is hard to distort, rather than at an account of the work, which is easy to.
Turn it on yourself. Your own trackers are subject to this. A number you grade yourself against gets gamed by the same mechanism, quietly.
In practice
Room 148Marks awarded for visible compliance produce visible compliance.Look at the writing rather than at whether the steps were followed. The work is the artefact; the process check is the report.
Forge OSA tick box you set for yourself becomes a thing to tick.This is why mushin sits outside the enough count — a practice about not evaluating yourself can’t come with a way to fail.
Block BAsking host teachers for a status summary gets you a summary.Being in the room for twenty minutes gets you the situation.
Failure signal: the reports are consistently good and the results aren’t moving. The reporting has detached from the thing it was meant to describe.
Appreciation before instructionDOCTRINE
Get the real picture from the people holding it before you decide anything. They are closer to the situation than you are, always.
Use when you are about to set direction on something other people are actually doing.
Ask what you are missing, not whether the plan is good. The second question gets agreement; the first gets information.
Go to where the work is. A picture assembled from reports is assembled from things people chose to tell you.
Take the awkward answer seriously in front of them. What you do with the first uncomfortable thing you hear sets the price of telling you the second.
In practice
Room 148Ask a class what part of the task was unclear before redesigning it.“Did that make sense?” gets nods. “Which part would you explain differently?” gets the answer.
Block BThe three host teachers have a picture of those students you cannot get any other way.Appreciation first; the support model follows from it rather than being designed and then fitted.
HomeBefore planning something for one of the girls, find out where she actually is now.A plan built from your picture of her is built from a picture that is some months old.
Failure signal: nobody has told you anything surprising in a long time. That means either nothing is happening or nothing is reaching you, and the second is far more likely.
Build the connection in advanceDOCTRINE
What lets dispersed people act coherently is a shared picture built beforehand, not messages sent during.
Use when things are calm. That is the only time this can be built; in the moment it is too late.
Say what matters most, repeatedly, before it is needed. People can only make your call in your absence if they know what you are optimising for.
Be predictable. Shared orientation requires that people can model you. Inconsistency forces everyone back to asking.
Spend the calm time on the relationship, not the system. The connection is what carries load later; the process document is not.
In practice
Room 148Criteria taught early and repeated are what let students work when you are with someone else.A class that knows what good work looks like doesn’t queue at your desk.
FLA 6/7Adults manage themselves once they know what the work is for and what the run of dates is.The presentation schedule in front of them all term is exactly this move — the picture held in advance, not chased.
HomeThe household version is agreeing what the evening is for before it starts.Most domestic friction is parts working against each other without a shared picture, not disagreement.
Failure signal: things work when you are present and fall apart when you are not. The connection was never built; you were the connection.
Stop being the nodeC
Anything that routes back to you for a decision is an intent that was never stated clearly enough for someone else to act on.
Use when you feel like the bottleneck, or when you notice you are the only one who knows something.
Treat each question as a diagnostic. Don’t just answer it — note what the person would have needed in order not to ask.
Give the boundaries rather than the answer. What outcome, by when, what must not happen. Then the next twenty questions answer themselves.
Connect people to each other, not to you. A hub is slower than a mesh, and it is also fragile in exactly the way you are: it fails when you are tired.
Notice where this serves you. Being the necessary node is quietly satisfying, and that is worth being honest about, because it is a reason to keep the design that way.
In practice
Room 148A queue at your desk is a criteria problem, not a behaviour problem.Post what finished looks like and the queue shortens without a single instruction.
Block BIf every arrangement runs through you, the three teachers cannot coordinate without you.Put them in contact with each other about the shared students.
HomeBeing the only one who knows the schedule is a bottleneck you built.Visible and shared beats remembered and relayed.
Failure signal: you are tired and things are waiting on you. That combination is a design outcome, not bad luck.
Make true reports survivableB
The receiving half. How you react to bad news determines the quality of everything you are told afterwards.
Use when someone tells you something you don’t want to hear — and that is the whole trigger, because the reaction is the move.
Respond to the message, not the messenger. Teams where people can raise problems without cost perform measurably better, and the mechanism is simply that the problems arrive early enough to fix. B
Thank the person who brought it before doing anything about it. Cheap, and it is the whole price signal.
Say your own misses out loud. People calibrate what is sayable from what you say about yourself.
Watch the delay, not the content. How long it took someone to tell you is the honest measure of how safe telling you is.
In practice
Room 148A student saying the instructions were confusing is doing you a favour.If that costs them anything, the next thirty students stay quiet and you teach the unit blind.
IncomePilot users telling you a module is confusing are the most valuable input you have.Explaining why they misread it converts a signal into a reassurance.
HomeThe version that matters most and is hardest: what happens when one of the girls tells you something awkward.The reaction to the first one sets the price of the next ten years of them.
Failure signal: you find things out late. Not because nobody knew — because telling you had a price.
Start this week
1
Pick one check or tracker you run and ask what it costs the person being checked. Yourself counts.
2
Ask one class or one colleague “what am I missing?” rather than “does this make sense?”
3
The next time someone asks you a question, note what they’d have needed not to ask it — then supply that instead of only the answer.
4
Find one thing only you know and make it visible rather than relayed.
5
Next time you hear something you don’t want to hear, thank them first, before responding to the substance.
The Sunday block
Four questionsweekly · 5 min
What did someone tell me this week that I didn’t want to hear — and how did I take it?
What did I find out later than I could have?
What waited on me this week, and what would have let it not wait?
Has anyone surprised me recently? If not, why not?
Not wired into the debrief. Read them in the existing Sunday review.
The one thing
What would they have needed in order not to ask me? Supply that, and stop being the node.
Failure modes
Delegating without the intent. Handing over tasks while keeping the picture produces people who ask more, not fewer, questions.
Mistaking silence for safety. A quiet room is evidence of nothing. The measure is whether awkward things reach you early, not whether complaints reach you at all.
Replacing monitoring with a better tracker. The paradox applies to the replacement too. The answer is looking at the work, not a smarter instrument.
Building the process instead of the connection. Documents don’t carry load in a difficult moment; the relationship does, and it has to exist beforehand.
Liking being needed. The hub design has a payoff, which is why it persists. Worth naming when you catch yourself defending it on other grounds.
Evidence
DOCTRINE — Boyd’s own synthesis, drawn from his reading of German practice and his study of organisations under pressure. Influential and widely taught, but interpretation rather than an empirical finding.
A — strong, replicated experimental support. Nothing on this page is graded A.
B — good supporting research, narrower in scope: measurement distortion, where any measure used for judgement stops measuring what it did (Campbell; Goodhart), and psychological safety, where teams that can surface problems without cost perform better (Edmondson). Autonomy over method sits here too.
C — reasoned from your own records and this system’s history rather than from research. Stop being the node is in this class.
D — weak or contested. Nothing on this page is graded D.
Boyd delivered this briefing in 1987 as the section directly after Patterns of Conflict. He never published; the slides are terse because he talked over them.

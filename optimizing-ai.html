Optimizing AI

Ten questions that pull the most valuable knowledge out of an AI model. The highest-value things a model holds are patterns across thousands of cases, the names of things, and how systems really work. It gives up very little of that unless you ask.

Reasoned, not researched. This list is Claude's judgment about where AI adds the most value. It carries no evidence grades because no study ranks these moves; treat the ordering as a strong opinion.
1How things like this usually fail
Your plan is rarely the first of its kind. The model has read thousands of accounts of plans like it going wrong.
Failure patterns repeat across a type of project far more reliably than success patterns do. Knowing the usual three ways it dies is cheap insurance before you commit.
The prompt
What are the most common ways projects like this fail, and which am I most exposed to?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, builder, money
Teacher
Launching a new classroom routine in the first weeks at Richmond High.
What are the most common ways a new classroom routine falls apart by October, and which is my setup most exposed to?
Builder
Before starting a new Forge build.
How do personal automation systems like this usually get abandoned, and what in my design invites that?
Money
Before trusting a backtested signal with real money.
What are the typical ways a backtested scoring system fails once it goes live, and which could my validation test miss?
2The questions you didn't know to ask
An expert's first move is usually a question the beginner never thought of. Ask the model to supply it.
Most bad advice comes from answering the question as asked. This move surfaces the missing context before any answer is given, so everything after it is better aimed.
The prompt
What would an expert in this field ask me before giving advice?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, builder, consumer
Teacher
A unit plan that feels finished.
What would an experienced ELL department head ask me about this unit before approving it?
Builder
About to automate something in the brief.
What would a senior engineer ask me before I build this?
Consumer
Signing up for a phone plan, lease or insurance renewal.
What questions would a savvy buyer ask before signing this?
3The strongest case against your view
People ask AI to help carry out a decision far more often than to attack it. The attack is where the value is.
Models lean toward agreeing with whoever is asking. An explicit request to argue the other side overrides that lean and gives you the objection you would otherwise meet too late.
The prompt
Argue as hard as you can that this is the wrong move.
When to use it 3 examples · money, writer, career
Money
A position or pick you are sure of.
Make the strongest bear case against this position, using only the facts I have given you.
Writer
A chapter you think is done.
Argue that this chapter's central claim is wrong, the way a hostile reviewer would.
Career
Your plan for replacing the teaching salary.
Argue that my plan for building non-teaching income is on the wrong track, and tell me what evidence would prove you right.
4The name for the thing
Once a problem has a name, decades of other people's work on it become searchable.
A vague problem can only be solved from scratch. A named one comes with research, known fixes and known traps. The name is often the single most valuable word in the answer.
The prompt
Is there a technical term for this, and what does the research say about it?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, builder, health
Teacher
A student reads aloud fluently but can't say what happened in the text.
Is there a name for a student who decodes well but doesn't comprehend, and what does the research suggest doing about it?
Builder
You keep rebuilding systems instead of using them.
Is there a name for the pattern of redesigning a system instead of running it, and what is known about breaking it?
Health
Something you notice in your sleep or training that you can't put a label on.
What's the technical term for what I'm describing, so I can look up the research myself?
5Solutions from other fields
Your problem has probably already been solved somewhere you would never think to look.
Fields reinvent each other's solutions constantly. The model has read across all of them at once, which no single specialist has.
The prompt
Which other fields have faced a structurally similar problem, and how did they solve it?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, builder, money
Teacher
Transitions between activities eat too much class time.
Who else has to get thirty people to switch tasks quickly on cue, like stage managers or flight crews, and how do they do it?
Builder
The brief shows so many alerts that you've started ignoring them.
How do hospitals and aviation handle alarm fatigue, and what can I borrow for a personal dashboard?
Money
A betting or fantasy decision under heavy uncertainty.
How do professional poker players or actuaries make decisions under this kind of uncertainty?
6How institutions actually work
Outcomes often turn on procedural levers most people never use: appeal routes, clauses, escalation paths, what a rep is allowed to do.
This is where the return is most direct, in money or hours. It is also where models are most likely to be out of date, so it pairs with the check-the-specifics rule below.
The prompt
What are the formal and informal levers in this process that most people don't use?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, consumer, money
Teacher
A question about prep time, class size or composition.
What provisions in a BC teachers' collective agreement typically govern this, and how do teachers normally raise it?
Consumer
A charge on a bill you think is wrong.
What is the full escalation path for disputing a charge with a Canadian telecom, and what are they actually allowed to do for me?
Money
Tax time with a salary plus side income.
What CRA rules and deductions commonly apply to a salaried teacher who also earns income from books and courses?
7Consensus versus popular advice
In health, money, parenting and learning, what everyone repeats and what the evidence supports are often different things.
Separating settled from contested stops you from building a routine on a claim that was never solid. It also tells you where your own judgment is genuinely needed.
The prompt
What's settled, what's genuinely contested, and where does common advice diverge from the evidence?
When to use it 3 examples · health, teacher, home
Health
A supplement or protocol that's everywhere online.
On this supplement, what's settled, what's contested, and where does popular advice outrun the evidence?
Teacher
A teaching practice colleagues swear by.
Which popular teaching practices have weak evidence behind them, and what does the research support instead?
Home
A parenting question with loud opinions on every side.
What does research actually settle about this for children, and what is still genuinely unknown?
8What practitioners know
The rules of thumb experienced people use are rarely taught. They show up mainly when practitioners write about their mistakes.
This compresses years of other people's trial and error into a short list. It is most useful at the start of anything, when you cannot yet tell which mistakes are expensive.
The prompt
What do people with twenty years in this wish they'd known in year one?
When to use it 3 examples · teacher, writer, career
Teacher
Your first high-school year in fifteen years.
What do teachers who returned to high school after a long gap wish they'd known in the first month?
Writer
Before drafting a nonfiction book.
What do authors who built a real readership for nonfiction wish they'd known before writing the first draft?
Career
Before launching a course.
What do people who have sold online courses wish they'd known before launch?
9The cheapest test that would settle it
Before you spend time or money, find the smallest experiment that tells you whether the belief underneath is true.
Gate expensive action behind cheap verification. The model is good at designing the test, even when it can't answer the question itself.
The prompt
What's the smallest, cheapest experiment that would tell me whether this is true?
When to use it 3 examples · builder, career, teacher
Builder
Before building an analysis page.
Before I build this, what's the cheapest test that shows whether my data can actually answer the question?
Career
Before writing a book or course.
What's the smallest test that would tell me whether anyone wants this before I write it?
Teacher
Before redesigning a whole unit.
What one-lesson test would tell me whether this approach works with these students?
10Patterns in your own thinking
The model can read everything you've said in a conversation and point out what you keep assuming without examining it.
You can't see your own assumptions, for the same reason you can't see your own face. The model supplies the missing view, and it works best late in a long conversation.
The prompt
Based on what I've said, what am I taking for granted?
When to use it 3 examples · builder, writer, career
Builder
At the end of a long planning session.
Based on everything I've said in this conversation, what am I taking for granted?
Writer
You have several drafts in front of you.
What do these pieces have in common that I might not be seeing?
Career
A decision you have explained several times.
Which of my stated reasons sound like the real reason, and which sound like justification?
Check the specifics
Learn which levers exist from the model. Confirm the details from the source.
Moves 6 and 7 are where a model is most likely to sound confident and be wrong or out of date: exact rules, clause numbers, deadlines, dosages, current policy. Use the answer to find out what to look for, then check it with the district, the CRA, the provider, or the primary study before you act.
What in your answer should I verify, and where would I check it?
For a big decision
One order that works when a decision matters. It's a suggestion, not a rule.
1Name it (move 4), so you are working on a known problem.
2Ask the expert's questions (move 2), and answer them.
3Get the failure modes (move 1) for plans of this type.
4Hear the case against (move 3) once your plan has taken shape.
5Run the cheapest test (move 9) before committing anything expensive.
The one thing
Before asking for an answer, ask what an expert would ask you.

It opens the door to most of the other moves, because the expert's questions surface the failure modes, the missing names and the institutional levers on their own. If you only remember one prompt from this page, remember that one.

Which move?

Recognition, not recall. Each prompt is a situation. Say aloud which move fits and roughly what you'd type, then reveal. Ten situations per round, drawn at random.

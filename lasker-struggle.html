Lasker: Struggle

After Emanuel Lasker, Struggle (New York, 1907; the German edition is Kampf). World chess champion for twenty-seven years, doctor of mathematics, and the first person to try to write one science of every contest. The companion to the Macheeide Protocol audio.

The idea
A position is worth what a perfect player could take from itDOCTRINE
Lasker’s claim was that a war, a lawsuit, a business, a game and a body fighting a disease all obey the same handful of laws. His definition of a struggle is the cleanest one available: something alive wants to reach a purpose against resistance. The resistance can be an opponent, but it can just as easily be nature, habit or your own body.
It is an old reflection that life is a struggle.Struggle, 1907 · the opening line
To measure any struggle he imagined a perfect fighter, the macheeide, from the Greek for a fight. It is not a person. It is a ruler, the way physics uses a frictionless plane: nothing is frictionless, but friction can’t be measured without the idea. The macheeide’s move in any position is uniquely determined, because every perfect strategist would choose the same one.
So the question is never “am I winning?” It is “what is this position actually worth, and am I taking what it offers?”
The vocabulary is the toolDOCTRINE
Lasker invented words so the same word would work on the board, in court and on a battlefield. Seven of them carry the whole book.
Machee
Any struggle, from war to art to business.
Stratoi
The forces, the centres of effect. In Room 148: your attention, a relationship with a student, the routine you built.
Field
The ground and its conditions. Change the field and every stratos changes value.
Energy
What gets spent: time, attention, goodwill, effort. The machee’s money.
Armoostia
Adaptability: how fast a force can change what it is doing.
Pressure
A threat that forces the other side to spend energy parrying it. It doesn’t have to land.
Amachic
An action that wastes energy. Its opposite, the macheeide’s choice, is eumachic.
Where this applies. Lasker’s machee is wider than Boyd’s conflict: the resistance doesn’t have to be a person. That makes most of this page safe in the classroom and the house, with one rule: there, the thing you are struggling against is the task, the habit or the condition, never the student or your daughter. Move three’s attack language belongs only where someone really is working against you — income, the stock and betting systems, a genuine dispute. Patterns of Conflict carries the same scope line for the same reason.
The five moves
01Work — best unit on what mattersDOCTRINE
A group’s value is its capacity to produce work, and the macheeide always sends its highest-value group to the most important task.
Use when you are busy and nothing is moving.
Name the most important task in this machee right now. One sentence.
Name your highest-value unit. Your best hour of attention, your strongest relationship, your most liquid capital.
Check whether they are on the same thing. Usually they aren’t; the best unit is on whatever looks urgent.
Every manoeuvre adds effect or moves toward greater effect. Lasker calls movement for its own sake Rococo: Frederick the Great’s armies marching for seasons and deciding nothing.
In practice
Room 148Your undivided attention is the highest-value unit in the room.Marking is where Rococo hides: visible output in a volume that feels like achievement.
HomeAn evening with the girls is your best unit at home.Spending it tired, on logistics, is manoeuvring.
IncomeThe stock system and Self-Evidence are both stratoi.Which one is this quarter’s most important task, and is the best unit on it, or split so neither gets work done?
Failure signal: a full week and the aim did not move. Lasker’s own note: society tends to reward the appearance of capacity rather than the work.
02Economy — defend only when forcedDOCTRINE
An action that wastes energy is amachic. Mediocrity acts on eagerness and impatience; the master waits.
Use when you feel the urge to respond, fix, answer or act right now.
Ask: am I actually forced? If not, hold. Pressure that costs nothing to ignore costs something to answer.
If forced, parry with the least that suffices. The wall is as strong as it needs to be and no stronger.
Don’t grab the gain in front of you before checking whether a better one is available.
Cheap prevention is not defending early. Nimzowitsch’s prophylaxis — stop the plan before it becomes a threat — is economical when it costs little. Lasker’s target is the expensive reaction to a threat that hasn’t arrived. C
Use complexity as a detector. Lasker: simplicity is economy, excess reads as affectation, and complicated behaviour usually hides a second motive. Your own elaborate plan included. C
In practice
Room 148A student pushes. Notice whether anything is actually threatened.If it is, a look, a name or a quiet word, then back to work. Engaging the push at full strength is the amachic move.
Daily lifeThe driver who cuts you off, the email with an edge on it, the last-minute request.Not forced means do not move.
TrainingThe minimum effective dose is economy.The extra sets that feel like commitment are often Rococo.
Failure signal: the regretted reply. Almost every one is a defence of something that was never under threat.
03Advantage — attack only from superiorityDOCTRINE
In a balanced position neither side can make progress. Advantage means an attack that has no sufficient defence, and superiority is the condition for attack.
Use when you are about to attack, or are being attacked.
Name which box you are in before you move.
Name the advantage you actually hold. Numerical (more force) or positional (better placement of the same force). If you can’t name it, you are attacking from balance, which the macheeide never does.
Stronger: attack the weakest point, not the most annoying one. Keep the pressure continuous. A slight edge justifies calm, patient aggression without the anxiety of a balanced position. Trading to fatigue an opponent can pay.
Weaker: seek compensating position. Move toward the least pressure. Don’t meet a high-adaptability attacker head-on; bind it with a continuous ring of pressure. Protect the fragile, high-value pieces first.
Changing the field is the weaker side’s move. Cheng · Ch’i holds the sixteen domains to change it into; this page doesn’t repeat them.
In practice
Room 148A settled class with an established routine is a positional advantage.That is when to push the harder content, calmly and continuously. An unsettled class makes you the weaker side: change the activity, pace or grouping until an advantage exists. The resistance is the room’s state, not a student.
Stock systemA slight statistical edge, held with patience.The urge to abandon it after one bad week is the anxiety of a balanced position wrongly applied to a slightly favourable one.
IncomeAgainst a bigger competitor, don’t contest their strongest point.Move to the least pressure: the ground they don’t cover.
Failure signal: you attacked and got refuted. Check whether there was any advantage to attack from.
04Chance — decide by the figureA
When causes are too many to compute, assign probabilities from observed frequency, then decide by the probable gain, not by inclination or vague fear.
Use when an outcome is unknown, and especially when the choice feels exciting or frightening.
Examine the situation.
List every possible manoeuvre, not the two that came to mind first.
Compute each one’s average gain across the likely outcomes. Rough probability times rough payoff is enough.
Ruin check first. If one outcome can knock you out of the game, average gain is the wrong target: size the stake so you survive the bad run (the Kelly criterion, 1956). Lasker hinted at this with “modest values”; the page makes it a step. A
Choose the highest probable profit. For any venture, compare it against the other uses of the same capital, labour and time.
Refusing good bets is waste too. A one-in-ten shot at eight units against a certain loss of one is a bad bet; declining a favourable risk because the downside is unpleasant to picture is the same error in reverse.
No system beats an edge against you. Doubling after losses and raising on streaks only change when you lose. Lasker calls the gambler’s system a fetish that resists criticism.
In practice
IncomeAny venture gets the four steps before it gets the hours.A course launch, a trading rule, a side build: compare its probable gain with the alternative use of the same summer.
Betting & fantasyThe system you won’t audit is the fetish.Lasker would ask for the record.
Others’ riskHis moral line: staking your own modest values is merely imprudent.Knowingly pushing other people into unprofitable risks is the real wrong.
Failure signal: the decision was made by the feeling, and the arithmetic was done afterwards to justify it.
05Justice — attack to the position’s worthRECONSTRUCTED
Between competent sides, results tend to match the real advantages held. Claim more and a sound defence refutes you; claim less and the advantage decays.
Use when you are tempted either to overreach or to sit on a gain.
Ask what the macheeide would get from this position. That is the line.
Attack to it, not past it. An attack that exceeds what the position contains is unjust in Lasker’s structural sense, and it gets refuted.
Convert held advantage now. This is Steinitz’s chess law — the side with the advantage must attack or lose it — raised by Lasker into a law of every struggle.
Every unit acts for the whole. The chapter opens with a battle broken into many small contests: each unit engages where it does the most work, the unengaged wait as reserves, and an unforeseen jump gets reported to the commander at once.
In practice
Room 148A unit that landed and a class that is ready is held advantage.The next lesson has to take it. A soft week as a reward lets it decay.
HomeGoodwill with a teenager is positional advantage with a short half-life.Spent on something real, or lost.
IncomeOverreach and underreach both show up as pricing.Charge past what the evidence supports and you are refuted; charge less and you leave the position unconverted.
Failure signal: the quiet one — a won position, rested on.

Applied to me — time and focus
Match the best time to the most important taskC
Clyde’s reading of the five moves against my own situation. Reasoning, not research — and the calls are mine.
Rank the time. Summer weeks are the most valuable unit I own — the only stretch long enough to finish anything. The two spare hours a week in term are worth less, and less again at 10 p.m. Tired evening scraps are worth almost nothing for building. Best unit to the main effort; minor units to minor tasks.
This term I am the side defending. A new job in survival mode is a holding position, and Lasker’s weaker side doesn’t attack — it holds with the least that suffices and builds toward an advantage. So the term’s main effort is the one that raises the value of every other hour without needing my best thinking: getting back in shape (The Return and Be Like Mike already exist for it). Self-Evidence is the summer’s main effort. In term it gets capture only: observations and notes from Room 148 and from my own experiments, so the material piles up while I hold.
The job is the defence. Hold it with the least that works. Marking shortcuts, fast-marking test design and reusable deck generators are economy of defence, not side tasks — every hour they save goes to the main effort. Polishing a lesson that already works is defending what was never threatened.
The Rococo test for anything new. A new protocol, page or tool counts as work only if it serves the main effort or becomes content for Self-Evidence. Otherwise it is skilled manoeuvring, however good it is.
The summer is held advantage. Decide in June what it must produce — one output. Safety is the calm of a balanced position, and nobody progresses from balance. Every unspent week is advantage decaying.
Decide by the figure, with a ruin check. List the summer’s options, rough odds, rough payoff, and pick the highest. The exit itself gets the ruin check: replacement income proven in numbers before I resign, because that is the one bet that can end the game.
Attack from the edges I actually hold. Twenty years of ELL, three years of supervised action research, a curriculum that put me in front of rooms across Japan, and the ability to build tools fast. Self-Evidence stands on the action-research edge. Projects with no edge are where my hours buy least.
The master waits — on reactions, not on decisions. Once the figure is on paper, more thinking is the inclination Lasker warns about, not patience.
Killer instinct is a rule, not a personality. Name the advantage, convert it, keep the pressure on until it’s taken.
Unfairness is information about the field. When something feels unjust, ask what it says about the position: something I didn’t count, an advantage I don’t hold, the wrong field.
Weekly check: Did my best hour go to the main effort? Which finished piece is sitting unconverted?
What held up
A forerunner, corrected in four placesAC
Lasker’s idea of one formal theory for every contest came true, but it was built by mathematicians, not by him. Robert Leonard’s history of game theory (Cambridge, 2010) opens with Struggle; the German national biography calls the macheeide a forerunner of modern game theory. The corrections:
The macheeide sometimes varies. In games where both sides see everything, perfect play is fixed (Zermelo, 1913). Where information is hidden — a bluff, a bargain, a hand of cards — von Neumann proved in 1928 that the perfect player often mixes moves in set proportions. Predictability is itself a weakness. A
Chance needs a ruin check. Expected value is right for repeated bets; when one loss can end you, size the stake so you survive the bad run (Kelly). And people don’t naturally decide by the figure: losses weigh roughly twice as much as equal gains (Kahneman & Tversky, 1979), which is exactly the bias Lasker was arguing against. A
Economy has a partner. Prophylaxis — cheap prevention before a threat forms — arrived within twenty years. It refines “don’t defend until forced” rather than replacing it. C
Advantage is wider than he counted. Engines routinely give up material for time and initiative. Superiority still precedes attack, but initiative counts as superiority. C
Justice is the weakest. Results track advantage only over the long run between competent players; top-level chess is mostly draws, and big edges are thrown away under the clock. Keep “don’t sit on an advantage” as practice, not as a law. C
Lasker and Boyd
Lasker gives the ruler; Boyd gives the tempoC
They agree that strategy is one subject across every field; that the weaker side wins by changing the ground rather than matching force; that economy and simplicity are survival, not taste; and that defence done well is cheap while attack done badly is ruinous.
They part on continuity. Lasker’s positions change smoothly, advantage is measured, and justice means the result matches the advantage. Boyd’s world is discontinuous by design: a fast transient is exactly the jump Lasker treats as an exception the commander must hear about. Lasker tells you what a position is worth. Boyd tells you how to make the other side misjudge it. You need both.
Where Lasker is ahead: Boyd never wrote decision arithmetic. Lasker did, in 1907, and it still holds.

Failure modes
Busyness mistaken for work. Rococo feels like effort and produces nothing. The test is whether the aim moved.
Defending before you are forced. The commonest amachic act, and the one behind most regretted replies.
Attacking from balance. Impatience makes you attack when no advantage exists, and a sound defence punishes it.
Sitting on an advantage. Won and not converted, decaying while you rest.
The fetish. A system, belief or method you won’t audit because it has become identity rather than arithmetic.
Turning the people into the opponent. In Room 148 and at home the resistance is the task or the habit. The moment it becomes the student or your daughter, the page has been misread.
Evidence
DOCTRINE — Lasker’s own generalization from chess: the macheeide, the principles of work, economy and advantage, and the vocabulary. Read from the 1907 English text. It is a philosopher’s ruler, not a tested theory; its test is whether applying it improves your own results, which is an action-research question.
A — settled science. Move four’s core: decide repeated bets by expected value; martingale and streak systems cannot beat a house edge; avoiding small favourable risks is itself a cost.
C — reasoned. Economy as beauty has partial support in processing-fluency research (easy-to-process things are rated truer and more attractive), but “complexity hides a second motive” is an untested heuristic. The Lasker–Boyd comparison is Clyde’s reading, not scholarship.

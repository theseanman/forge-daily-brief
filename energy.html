Energy

Two jobs. Keep the everyday level high and still have it at 80. Raise it on purpose when a moment deserves it, then pay it back. Sleep and Longevity own the foundations; this page points to them rather than repeating them.

How energy actually works
Two things share one word
Capacity is the machinery: your aerobic engine, your muscle, your sleep and the absence of anything medically wrong. It changes over weeks and years.
Felt energy is a reading your brain takes, moment to moment, about whether effort is worth spending right now. It changes in minutes, and it responds to movement, light, cold, music, food, meaning and what you tell yourself.
The baseline half of this page builds capacity. The surge half moves the reading. The two need different tools, and mixing them up is how people end up using coffee to fix a problem that is really sleep.
Tiredness is not a fuel gaugeX
The popular model says willpower is a tank that empties as you use it. The largest test of it, a preregistered replication across 23 labs, found an effect of d = 0.04, close to zero. ✓
That does not mean fatigue is imaginary. It means the feeling of being spent is partly a forecast, not a measurement. That is why ten minutes of movement can make you less tired than ten minutes on the couch. Treat tiredness as a reading worth checking, not a verdict.
What to protect for old ageB
Researchers measure fatigability: how tired a standard task makes you. In 2,906 adults aged 60 and over, those scoring 25 or higher on the Pittsburgh Fatigability Scale were 2.3 times more likely to die over about 2.7 years, after adjusting for depression, illness and age. ✓
That is observational, so it does not prove fatigability causes death. But it names the target. The goal is not feeling buzzed at 47. It is a body that a walk up a hill, a grandchild or a full day does not flatten at 80, and that is built mostly by the aerobic and muscle work below.
Part one · The baseline
01Move most days, mostly easyA
The most reliable everyday energy lever there is, and it works faster than fitness does.
A meta-analysis of 70 randomized trials (6,807 people) found regular exercise raised energy and cut fatigue by 0.37 standard deviations; over 90% of the trials pointed the same way. ✓ A follow-up trial in 36 sedentary, chronically tired adults found low-intensity sessions (20 minutes, three times a week, six weeks) cut fatigue by 65%, more than moderate intensity did at 49%, and the gains were not tied to fitness improving. B ✓
The floor: 20 easy minutes on days you don't lift. Not a workout; a dose.
Easy counts. The energy effect does not need you to suffer. Save hard efforts for the plan that asks for them.
The training week itself lives in Longevity and Size and Strength. This move is the minimum underneath them, not a replacement.
Room 148Lunch is a 15-minute loop of the halls or the field, not a desk lunch on marking days.
HomeAn after-dinner walk with whoever will come. It counts as the dose and as family time.
Long dayOn a Tuesday or Thursday, ten minutes outside between school and the evening class beats ten minutes in the car.
02Build the engine you'll need at 80B
Mitochondria and muscle are the long-term bank account. Both still respond late in life.
In a Mayo Clinic study of 72 adults, high-intensity interval cycling raised muscle mitochondrial capacity by 49% in people aged 18–30 and by 69% in people aged 65–80. ✓ The older muscle had more to recover, and it recovered. It is a small study, so it is graded B.
Intervals once a week keep the engine's ceiling high. Your Longevity page already sets the dose.
Muscle is reserve: every everyday task costs a smaller share of a bigger capacity, so it feels easier. The Size and Strength plan is doing this job now.
Measure it. A yearly stair or hill time, or a VO₂max estimate, tells you whether the engine is holding. Feelings will lie about slow decline.
TrainingIntervals on a cardio day; lifting on plan days. Neither needs to be added; both need to survive the school year.
OutTake stairs as a test, not a chore. Note how you feel at the top, and compare next year.
03Rhythm before tricksA
Sleep is the base, and the Sleep page owns it. One point belongs here because it is about energy.
In a controlled study, 400 mg of caffeine taken six hours before bed cut measured sleep by more than an hour, and the people taking it did not notice. B ✓ Caffeine borrowed for energy today is paid back from tomorrow's energy, without you seeing the bill. That is why the 14:00 hard stop on the Sleep page is an energy rule as much as a sleep rule.
Fixed wake, morning light, 14:00 caffeine stop. See Sleep for the full version.
Dark mornings: from November, daylight before school is thin. Get it at lunch instead of skipping it.
Room 148Lunch outside under the overhang on grey days does the light job the morning can't.
HomeWeekend wake within half an hour of the weekday wake. A lie-in feels restoring and leaves Monday flatter.
04Fuel steady, drink earlyB
The "sugar rush" is mostly a sugar dip, and thirst arrives late.
A meta-analysis of 31 studies and about 1,300 adults found that people who had sugar felt more tired and less alert than those who didn't, not more energized. ✓ Separately, in healthy young men, losing about 1.6% of body weight in water raised fatigue on a standard mood scale. ✓ At your weight, 1.6% is roughly 3 lb, a deficit you can reach by mid-afternoon of a teaching day without feeling thirsty.
No sugar hit before a moment you need to be sharp. Protein with a carbohydrate beats a candy bar or a sweet coffee.
A water bottle on the desk, finished by lunch, refilled for the afternoon. Pale urine is the check.
Protein targets and meal timing live in Longevity and Size and Strength; this card is about the hour after you eat.
Room 148The staff-room treats on a Friday afternoon are a 30-minute dip, scheduled right before your last block.
Long dayBefore the evening class: a protein snack and water, not a muffin and a coffee.
HomeThe late-night snack is fine for the lifting plan. Keep it protein-led so it isn't a sugar spike before bed.
05Read the tiredness before obeying itC
Most afternoon flatness is under-stimulation or boredom wearing fatigue's clothes.
Because felt energy is partly a forecast, the test is cheap: do ten minutes of the thing, or ten minutes of movement, then check again. If you are still flat, the tiredness was real and the right answer is rest or an early night. If you've lifted, it was a forecast. The rule is reasoning built on the findings above, not a tested intervention, so it is graded C.
Name it: sleepy (eyes heavy, yawning), drained (body heavy after real effort) or bored (restless, reaching for the phone). Each wants a different fix.
Sleepy → nap or earlier bed. Drained → food, water, easy movement. Bored → change the task or raise the stakes.
BuildingFlat at the desk at 20:30. Ten minutes on the smallest piece of the build, then decide. Often the decision makes itself.
HomeToo tired to play with the girls. Start with five minutes of whatever they're doing. Bored-tired lifts; sleepy-tired doesn't, and then you say so honestly.
Room 148Marking at the end of the day feels impossible. A lap of the hallway first, then the first three papers only.
06Find the drainB
If fatigue lasts more than a few weeks with the basics in place, stop optimizing and get checked.
Longevity already lists the yearly labs. These are the ones a doctor looks at specifically for persistent fatigue. Ask for them by name:
Complete blood count and ferritin. Low iron in a man is uncommon and needs a reason found, not just a supplement. Iron overload is the other direction and causes fatigue too. Never take iron without a test; see Longevity.
TSH (thyroid), B12, vitamin D and A1c.
Morning testosterone if fatigue comes with low drive or lost strength. It has to be a morning draw to mean anything.
Mood. Low energy is one of the core signs of depression. A two-minute questionnaire (PHQ-9) is a normal part of a fatigue workup, not an accusation.
Sleep apnea. In one US estimate, 82% of men with sleep apnea are undiagnosed, and a 10% rise in body weight was linked to a sixfold rise in moderate-to-severe apnea. ✓ The STOP-Bang screen asks about snoring, tiredness, observed pauses in breathing, blood pressure, BMI over 35, age over 50, neck size and male sex. Being male already scores one point. Ask whoever shares your room whether you snore or stop breathing. Three or more points means asking your doctor about a sleep study.
Go now, don't wait for the routine visit: fatigue with unexplained weight loss, breathlessness, chest pain, night sweats, or a low mood that won't lift.
In the one strong trial of iron for unexplained fatigue (198 women with ferritin under 50), iron cut fatigue about 19 percentage points more than placebo. ✓ That was women. It is a reason to test, not a reason for a man to supplement.
Part two · The surge
Every surge is borrowed. The tools below raise the reading for an hour or two. Most of them cost something later: sleep, tolerance or recovery. So the first move is choosing where the surge goes, and the last move is paying it back. A surge every day stops being a surge.
S1Caffeine, dosed and timedA
The best-evidenced surge there is, and the easiest one to misuse.
The sports-nutrition position stand puts the effective range at about 3–6 mg per kg, with no added benefit at 9 mg/kg or more. ✓ At about 95 kg, 3 mg/kg is roughly 285 mg, about two strong coffees, and that is the ceiling for a physical effort. For mental sharpness, a smaller dose of about 100 mg usually does the job. B
Take it 30–60 minutes before the moment, not when you feel the dip.
Not after 14:00. That makes caffeine a morning and early-afternoon tool. On a Tuesday or Thursday, it is not the tool for the evening class.
Keep it special. Regular heavy use blunts the lift. How fast tolerance builds is genuinely debated C, but a lower everyday dose leaves more room for the days that matter.
Room 148Report-card day or a parent meeting at 11:00: one coffee at 10:15, not three across the morning.
TrainingA heavy Monday lift after school: about 200 mg at 13:30 is the latest that respects the 14:00 stop.
BuildingA Saturday build block: the one coffee goes in right before you sit down, not with breakfast an hour earlier.
S2The ten-minute napB
Shorter than you think, and the length is the whole trick.
After a short night, people napped at 15:00 for 5, 10, 20 or 30 minutes. The 10-minute nap worked best: benefits started immediately and some lasted up to 155 minutes. A 20-minute nap took about 35 minutes to start helping. A 30-minute nap left people groggy at first. Five minutes did little. ✓ It is a small lab study.
Set the timer for 15 minutes in total, which allows a few minutes to fall asleep.
Before 16:00, so it doesn't eat into the night.
Lying still with your eyes closed still helps if sleep doesn't come. Don't fight it.
Long dayBetween school and the evening class, parked in the car with the seat back: ten minutes, then walk in. This is the evening-class tool caffeine can't be.
HomeA weekend afternoon before the girls' ringette games: a short nap, not a long one that leaves you groggy at the rink.
Room 148A prep block after a short night: door closed, eyes shut, timer on.
S3Move firstB
The fastest legal lift with no bill attached.
A single bout of aerobic exercise, even 10 minutes, reliably raises energized, positive feelings for a while afterward. This is the acute side of the 70-trial finding in move 01. B It costs nothing later and can be done at any hour.
Ten minutes, brisk enough to breathe harder. Stairs, a fast walk, a few rounds on the bag.
Before you decide you're too tired (move 05), not instead of deciding.
Room 148Before the last block: two flights of stairs, then back to the room.
HomeFlat on a Sunday afternoon: three rounds on the BOB, then the thing you were avoiding.
OutParking at the far end of the lot before a long errand day is a free dose.
S4Cold, brieflyC
A real chemical jolt. The step from chemistry to better performance is the weak link.
In an hour of head-out immersion at 14°C, noradrenaline rose about 530% and dopamine about 250%. ✓ That physiology is solid. Whether a short cold shower makes you perform better is much less tested, so it is graded C. Use it as a wake-up, not a performance drug.
30–60 seconds of cold at the end of a shower, or cold water on the face and wrists.
Not straight after lifting; your rule already covers this. Know your blood pressure first (Longevity), because cold spikes it.
Long dayCold water on the face in the washroom before the evening class.
HomeA cold finish to the morning shower on a day you need to be switched on early.
TrainingAn ocean dip on a cardio day, never a lifting day.
S5Music with a pulseA
Well tested, cheap, and it works on mood more than on muscle.
A meta-analysis of 139 studies found music improved how good exercise felt (g = 0.48), physical performance (0.31) and perceived effort (0.22). ✓ The mood lift is the biggest effect, which is why it works outside the gym too.
Fast, loud, familiar, and chosen by you. Build one short playlist for "switch on" and keep it only for that.
Use it in the run-up, not during work that needs language. Lyrics compete with reading and writing.
Long dayThree tracks in the car before walking into the evening class.
TrainingThe switch-on playlist for the first working set only, so it keeps its edge.
HomeMusic on while cooking after a draining day changes the room for everyone, not just you.
S6Fuel the momentB
What you eat in the hour before matters more than what you ate yesterday.
No sugar hit in the hour before; move 04 explains why.
Water first. Half a litre 30–60 minutes out is the cheapest fix on the page.
For an hour-long hard physical effort, rinsing a sports drink around your mouth and spitting it out has improved performance in several trials, most clearly when fasted. B It is a niche tool, but it is legitimate.
Long dayGreek yogurt or a protein shake at 16:30 before the evening class, with water.
OutA long errand day: eat something with protein before you go, not a coffee-shop pastry halfway through.
S7Name the arousal as readinessC
The last 60 seconds before a moment are decided in your head.
A racing heart before something big feels like nerves, and nerves feel like a drain. Studies that told people to read that arousal as the body getting ready found better performance and steadier stress responses. Motivational self-talk has its own supportive literature. C Both effects are modest and have not been re-checked for this page. Use them as finishers, not foundations.
One line, said once: "This is fuel." Or your own warrior image of the job ahead.
If you need to come down, not up, use five minutes of cyclic sighing (two inhales through the nose, one long exhale). It beat mindfulness for mood in a one-month randomized study. B ✓ The Breathing Room carries the timer.
Room 148Walking to a tough class: "This is fuel," then the first instruction in a strong voice.
OutA hard conversation with a stranger, a contractor or a ref: one long exhale before you speak.
TrainingBefore a heavy set: the warrior image, then lift.
S8Pay it backD
Reasoning, not a study: the debt is real even when the research on repaying it is thin.
That night: no extra caffeine, lights out on time, no "I earned a late one."
Next day: easy movement only. The surge drew on the same reserve your training does.
Two surge days in a row is a warning sign. Three means you are covering a baseline problem; go back to Part one.

The one thing
Flat? Move for ten minutes before you decide. It builds the baseline, it is the cleanest surge, and it tells you whether the tiredness is real.

Failure modes
Coffee covering short sleep. Tomorrow's energy pays today's, and you won't notice the sleep loss.
Sugar for a lift. It delivers a dip inside the hour.
The 40-minute nap. Grogginess, then a worse night.
Surging every day. At that point the baseline is the problem.
Optimizing around a medical drain. Weeks of fatigue with the basics in place is a doctor's question.
Calling boredom fatigue. Change the task before you give up the evening.
Evidence key
A Meta-analyses or consistent trials. B Good trials or strong observational data, with limits named. C Plausible mechanism, thin or mixed outcome data. D Reasoning. X A popular claim that failed testing.

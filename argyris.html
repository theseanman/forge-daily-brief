What you say you believe, and what your behaviour is actually running on. Chris Argyris spent forty years on the gap between the two and on why capable, sincere people cannot see it in themselves. Eight instruments in the Work tab.

The gap
01Espoused theoryC
What you would say if asked what you believe and intend.
It is sincere. It is usually admirable. It is also the only thing introspection can reach, which is why asking yourself what you believe returns it every time and tells you nothing you did not already have.
02Theory-in-useC
The operating logic your behaviour actually runs on.
Inferred backwards from what you did, never reported directly. Argyris found that almost nobody's two theories match, and that the person holding them is the last to know. Other people can often see yours.
The instrument is never a question about yourself. It is a record of behaviour plus the question: what rule would have had to be running to produce that?
Why reading does nothing. A book, a workshop, a protocol page updates your espoused theory. The theory-in-use is untouched, because it was never built out of propositions in the first place. This is the mechanism behind "I know what to do and I don't do it" — not weakness, not akrasia. Two separate systems, only one of which you can edit by being told things.
Model I — the default
The four governing valuesD
Argyris claimed almost every theory-in-use he measured ran these four, across cultures and professions.
Stay in unilateral control. Define the purpose and keep hold of it.
Maximise winning, minimise losing. Treat changing your position as a loss.
Suppress negative feeling. Yours and theirs. Keep it pleasant.
Be rational. Appear objective; treat emotion as a lapse.
Each one is defensible on its own. Together they produce a person who cannot receive disconfirming information, because every route in has been closed for a good reason.
Skilled incompetenceD
Being very good at not finding out.
The competence is real — years of practice at smoothing, deflecting, easing in, keeping the room comfortable. It is skilled because it is fluent and automatic. It is incompetence because what it reliably produces is the absence of the information you needed.
Easing in is the signature move: asking leading questions designed to walk someone to a conclusion you already hold, rather than stating it and testing it. It feels kind. It is unilateral control wearing a soft coat.
The two loops
Single loopC
Detect the error, change the strategy, leave the goal alone.
A thermostat holding the room at 68. Most improvement is single-loop and most of it is correct — this is not the inferior option. It is simply unable to notice that you are solving the wrong problem well.
Double loopC
Turn and interrogate the governing value itself.
The thermostat asking why it is set to 68. Single loop feels like diligence; double loop feels like insubordination — including to yourself, which is most of why it is rare.
Room 148Single: reteach the unit, rebuild the worksheet, regroup the desks. Double: is the thing I am assessing the thing I actually want them to be able to do?
FLA 6/7Single: adjust the pacing of the presentation block. Double: is a presentation the right evidence of this outcome for adults in an evening class?
IncomeSingle: ship the next module faster. Double: is the course the vehicle, or is it the thing that feels like progress?
HomeSingle: get the work done earlier so there is time. Double: is "enough work done" the right test at all?
Defensive routines
The undiscussable, and its coverC
Practices that protect people from embarrassment and prevent the problem from being named.
Everyone in the room knows. Nobody says it. That is stage one and it is survivable. Stage two is what makes it permanent: the undiscussability is itself undiscussable. Naming the fact that nobody names it is the forbidden move, so the routine never comes up for review.
The diagnostic is cheap. What can you not say here? The boundary of what is sayable is the boundary of the frame, and it marks it more reliably than anything you can say.
Model II — the alternative
Three governing valuesD
Valid information. Including the information that makes you look bad.
Free and informed choice. The other person can genuinely decide otherwise.
Internal commitment, monitored. Held because it holds up, not because it was agreed — and checked again later.
In practice this comes down to pairing advocacy with inquiry: state your actual view, say what it rests on, then ask what they see that you do not — and mean it. Instrument 8 drafts this.
Honest grading. Argyris's reputation outruns his evidence. The body of work is case studies, consulting engagements and self-report, with very little controlled replication — hence the C and D grades above. The framework is durable and widely used; it is not a measured mechanism. What independently supports the underlying claim is listed next.
What does hold upB
Self-report diverges from behaviour. Self-evaluations correlate only modestly with objective performance measures (Mabe & West 1982 meta-analysis, corrected r ≈ .29). You are a weak instrument on yourself.
Intention is not the bottleneck. A meta-analysis of experimental studies found that a medium-to-large change in intention produced only a small-to-medium change in behaviour (Webb & Sheeran 2006). Updating what you believe does not move what you do — which is the gap in different language.
Introspective reports are reconstructions. People confidently explain choices by causes that demonstrably were not operating (Nisbett & Wilson 1977). Contested in its strong form; the weak form is secure and is all this page needs.
None of this proves Model I. It establishes the part that matters operationally: asking yourself is not a method, and a behavioural record is.
Scope
Where this does not go. This is an instrument for examining your own reasoning, not other people's. Running the Model I diagnosis on a colleague, a student or your daughters is exactly the unilateral-control move it is meant to catch, and it converts a tool into a weapon — you end up with a vocabulary for being right about people. The only parts that travel outward are advocacy-with-inquiry and asking what cannot be said here. Everything else points inward or it is being misused.
Hands off to Shaping and Adapting. That page already owns writing and scoring predictions about outcomes, the outside judge, and finding contradictions between what you claim and what the world returns. This page does not restate them. Instrument 6 here is deliberately narrower: predictions about your own conduct, which is the one case where you are the only available observer.

Failure modes
Collecting insights instead of records. If an entry contains no quoted speech and no number, it is your espoused theory with new vocabulary on it.
Explaining the left column. The urge to justify why your unsaid thought was warranted in that particular case is not an obstacle to the exercise. It is the exercise working, and stopping there wastes it.
Using it on other people. Covered in the scope band. The tell is that it starts feeling good.
Double-looping everything. Interrogating the governing value of every small decision is paralysis with a respectable name. Most things are single-loop and should stay there.
Treating the grades as higher than they are. C and D means use it as a lens, not as proof. If you cite this in the book, cite it as a framework.
Evidence key
A — replicated experiments or meta-analysis, effect holds.
B — good evidence, some contest at the edges.
C — coherent and widely used, thin controlled testing.
D — descriptive framework from case work; useful, unmeasured.

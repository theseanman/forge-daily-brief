The Making of a Strategist

Five elements that Boyd, Lasker, Musashi and Clausewitz shared before they were great, each with a ladder to build it and a plan to use it. The companion to the audio of the same name.

The idea
Five things they had before the theoryREASONING
Each of the four competed where someone else kept the score, stood outside the institution at a decisive moment, imported from a field that had nothing to do with strategy, practised long before writing, and aimed at the other side’s mind rather than its material.
The five are one loop. The arena produces verdicts. The record keeps them. The import supplies new things to try. The outside stops you accepting the standard answer in place of your own result. The mind is where the gains are largest once the other four are running.
The warning that comes first. Picking four famous strategists and asking what they share is survivorship reasoning. Plenty of outsiders with hard experience and wide reading became nothing. The elements may be necessary without being enough. The test is whether building one changes your own results — an action-research question. Treat each as a hypothesis with a log attached.
Four corrections from the fact checkHISTORY
Boyd’s arena was a training arena. Twenty-two missions in Korea as a wingman, never fired his guns. The record that made him was the forty-second bet at the Fighter Weapons School. The arena has to be honest, not mortal.
Boyd did not cite Musashi. He found the Japanese classics after his major briefings; Musashi is in none of Boyd’s own works, though the book became a favourite.
Musashi’s numbers are his own. First duel at thirteen and sixty without defeat both come from his own introduction.
Clausewitz is the weakest outsider. A career officer who made major general; the case rests on his 1812 resignation.
Where this applies. Elements one to four are safe everywhere. Element five carries the same scope line as Patterns of Conflict and Lasker: against a real opponent you aim at expectations; in Room 148 and at home there is no opponent, and the skill is used only to read and to lead. If the student or your daughter becomes the enemy, the page has been misread.
The five elements
01The ArenaAHISTORY
Compete, repeatedly, where someone else decides the result and the answer comes fast. Theory comes later.
The pattern. Lasker held the world title 1894–1921, twenty-seven years. Clausewitz entered the army at twelve and was in combat within about a year. Musashi, by his own account, fought from thirteen. Boyd had the forty-second bet.
Why it works. Kahneman and Klein (2009) agreed that skilled intuition forms only when the environment is regular enough to learn and the feedback is rapid and unambiguous. The arena is about calibration, not toughness.
Cultivate — stay on a rung until it is boring
Rung 1 · scored drillAttach a number you don’t control to something you do alone.Clean combinations landed in a three-minute round. Number written before you decide how it felt.
Rung 2 · predictionOne line before a lesson: what will the exit tickets show?Shaping and Adapting owns the full method. Use it; don’t rebuild it.
Rung 3 · low-stakes opponentPlay something against a person, to a result, weekly.Chess online, a board game with the girls played to win, a lineup call recorded before kickoff.
Rung 4 · live audienceFive minutes at an open mic as Doug Fistmore.Record it. Mark each joke laugh, smile or nothing — from the tape, not memory.
Rung 5 · hits backSupervised sparring or partner pads.Only when the body is ready and inside Be Like Mike’s rules.
Exploit
Trust fast judgment where you logged it, and only there. The log shows you the edge of your expertise. No log, slow down and use paper.
Teach from a record. “I ran this forty times” is a different product from “experts say.”
Lose the fear of the verdict. Scored often, a bad score stops being a statement about you. REASONING
Failure signal: you call a session good or bad before you look at the number. Or the number has stopped being kept.
Weekly minimum: one scored contest, one prediction checked.
02The OutsideHISTORYREASONING
Stand where you can’t lean on the institution’s answer, so you have to find out for yourself what works.
The pattern. Boyd retired a colonel in 1975 after years of Pentagon fights. Musashi was a ronin until 1640. Lasker was forced out of Germany in 1933 — England, Moscow in 1935, New York in 1937. Clausewitz resigned in 1812 and fought for Russia against the alliance his own king had signed.
Two chose it, one had it forced on him, one preferred it. What they share is not rebellion. It is a habit of checking.
Cultivate — quietly
Rung 1 · default auditWeekly, pick one thing you do because it is how it is done.What evidence do I have, from my own room or house, that this works? None is a candidate for a test, not a reason to drop it.
Rung 2 · quiet testOne audited default a month, run two ways.Two blocks of English 8, one measure chosen in advance. Tell no one until there is a result.
Rung 3 · outside judgeOne person outside teaching who sees your work regularly.Shaping and Adapting owns this move.
Rung 4 · separate groundIncome projects wholly outside the school’s approval and calendar.Their standard is whether a stranger would pay.
Exploit
Defaults are where the cheap gains are. One audited marking convention can return hours a term.
The outside is your market position. A method built by a working teacher who tested it on himself. Say so plainly.
Hold the job with less. Give it what it requires and no more — Lasker’s economy.
Failure signals: a month with no default questioned (gone native), or arguing with colleagues about how things ought to be (a habit turned into a cause). Boyd’s price is the warning.
Weekly minimum: one default audited, one line written.
03The ImportHISTORYREASONING
Bring something into the contest from a field that has nothing to do with it — its ideas and its standard of rigour.
The pattern. Boyd’s “Destruction and Creation” (1976) is built from Gödel, Heisenberg and the second law of thermodynamics. Lasker took a mathematics doctorate at Erlangen under Max Noether (sources give 1900 or 1902). Musashi was an ink painter of standing. Clausewitz and German philosophy is the usual claim and the weakest case.
Cultivate
Rung 1 · forced transferAfter anything from outside, one sentence in a fixed form.“In Room 148 this means…” “In the ring this means…” “In the book this means…” No sentence, it was entertainment.
Rung 2 · one field deepOne outside field per term, far enough to meet its rigour.Probability from the stock system, comedy writing, or boxing mechanics. One deep beats six toured.
Rung 3 · transfer testedOne written transfer a month, run and checked.Stand-up’s “cut every word before the punchline” applied to class instructions.
Rung 4 · reverse importCarry teaching outward.Twenty years of making hard language simple is a skill most self-improvement writers lack.
Exploit
Combinations that are hard to copy. Anyone can read Boyd. Few can join Boyd, action research and second-language teaching.
Explanation. Two fields means twice the stock of comparisons.
Early warning. When one field contradicts another, you have found an error or an opening.
Failure signal: a stack of interesting notes and no transfer sentences. Collecting has replaced importing.
Weekly minimum: one transfer sentence. Shaping and Adapting’s “destroy to create” is what this habit feeds.
04The RecordHISTORYREASONING
Practice first, book late. A record kept during the practice is what lets the theory be both true and teachable.
The pattern. Musashi finished the Book of Five Rings in 1645, weeks before he died. Clausewitz began On War in 1816 after two decades of service and never finished; Marie published it in 1832. Boyd never published a book — he revised briefings for years. Lasker is the partial exception: Struggle came in 1907, thirteen years into his reign.
Cultivate
Rung 1 · one-line logDated line per experiment: tried, predicted, happened.No essay. The line is the asset.
Rung 2 · earned claimNo principle goes into a chapter or page without five dated entries.Reading can suggest a principle. Only the log can license it.
Rung 3 · briefing before bookExplain a finding aloud before writing it.What the listener misunderstands is what you haven’t understood yet.
Rung 4 · revisionOnce a term, reread an old entry and mark what was wrong.Conceptual Spiral: give the frame an expiry.
Exploit
Authority that can be checked. It answers the reader’s quiet question: did the author do this himself?
A brake on fluency. You write fast and so does Clyde. A persuasive chapter about something untested is easy to produce.
Material. Two hours a week won’t write a book. It will keep a log. Capture in term, build in summer.
Failure signal: drafting a principle you can’t point to entries for. Or a new protocol page built with nothing logged against the last one.
Weekly minimum: three one-line entries.
05The MindHISTORYA
Move the contest from material to what the other side believes, expects and can bear.
The pattern. Boyd: physical, mental, moral — and the moral is deepest. Clausewitz: moral forces, will, friction. Musashi: rhythm, timing, moving the opponent as you wish. Lasker: “he played the man” is the popular summary; your Lasker page has the fuller account.
Settled. Von Neumann (1928): with hidden information, the best play often mixes its moves in fixed proportions. Predictability is an exploitable weakness.
Cultivate
Rung 1 · expectation lineBefore anything that matters: what do they expect me to do?Two weeks of noticing only. Check afterward whether you were right.
Rung 2 · deliberate breakOnce a day, break an expectation at no stakes.A class braced for a raised voice gets a lowered one. Watch what it does to attention.
Rung 3 · feintShow one punch, throw another. Write a punchline.A set-up builds an expectation; the last word breaks it.
Rung 4 · read under pressureHold the expectation line during a live negotiation or dispute.Think Like Boyd: model people by their constraints.
Rung 5 · mixFacing the same counterpart repeatedly, check your own predictability.Vary on purpose.
Exploit
Cheaper wins. Change what someone expects and you often don’t need force. A class that can’t predict your next move is paying attention. REASONING
Defence. You notice when your own expectations are being managed — by a salesman, an institution, a headline.
Writing. Set an expectation, then meet it slightly differently than promised.
Failure signals: surprised by a reaction one line of thought would have predicted. Or running this on your family as a tactic — the scope line crossed.
Weekly minimum: five expectation lines, checked afterward.
Start this month
1
Month one: Arena and Record only. One scored contest and one checked prediction a week, three one-line entries.
2
Add one element a month, and only if the log shows the last one is alive.
3
If only one, the Arena. It is the only element that corrects the other four. A record with no verdicts is a diary; an untested import is a hobby; an outsider with no results is a crank; mind-reading with no feedback is guessing.
4
The cheapest single rep: five minutes at an open mic trains Arena, Record, Import and Mind at once.
5
Do the drill once. Recognition before it has to be recall.
The Sunday block
Five questionsweekly · 5 min
Where did someone else keep the score this week, and what was it?
Which default did I question?
What did I carry in from outside, in one sentence?
How many lines went in the log?
Whose expectation did I read right, and whose did I miss?
Not wired into the debrief. Read them in the existing Sunday review.
The one thing
Find the place where someone else keeps the score, go there every week, and write down what happened.
Failure modes
Starting all five. The ladder is one element a month.
Reading in place of the arena. More strategy books feel like progress and produce no verdict.
The outsider as identity. Checking is the habit. Opposition is the trap.
Theory ahead of the log. A chapter written from fluency rather than entries.
Turning the people into the opponent. Element five stops at the classroom door and the front door.

Which element?

Recognition, not recall. Each prompt is a situation. Name the element being kept or broken — Arena, Outside, Import, Record or Mind — and the move. A few are scope or ladder calls. Nine per round, drawn from thirty-one, then the one thing.

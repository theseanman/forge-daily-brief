The Capital Protocol

Capture, don't beat. The edge isn't which securities you hold — it's cost, diversification, behaviour, and time. Optimise structure, not selection, and let it compound toward the exit.

1The Base Rate
You don't need to beat the market. Capturing it is nearly free — the main way people forfeit the return is by trying to do better.
The Index Default — the question is no longer "which stock or move" but "why would I do anything other than own the whole market cheaply?" Every deviation now carries a high burden of proof.
Verified or vibe? — before any specific bet, ask what you know that the market's aggregate of informed participants doesn't already know and price. You'll almost never clear it. That's the pillar working.
Kill the tip reflex — a hot tip gives a small hit of excitement. Do nothing with it. Not "research it later." Nothing.
Even full-time professionals mostly can't beat the market net of costs over time — ~92% of active US equity funds lagged over 20 years. The prior that you will, in your evenings after teaching, should be very low. That's liberating, not an insult.
2The Only Free Edge
You can't control returns. You almost fully control what you subtract — fees, tax, friction — and every dollar you don't subtract compounds for decades.
The total-cost audit — find your real all-in cost once: every MER, advisor fee, account fee, trading cost. Above ~0.5% for something you could index at 0.05–0.20% is free money on the floor.
The fee line — you don't buy a product whose MER you haven't looked up and can't justify against a broad index. "I'm not paying for that" is a complete sentence.
RESP grant first — $2,500 per child per year captures the 20% CESG, up to $500 free per child. A guaranteed, instant 20% before the market does anything.
Cost is one of the only reliable predictors of future return — and it predicts negatively. A 2% fee can quietly eat a third to a half of your final wealth. Verify current numbers against the CRA.
3The Free Lunch
How you split across asset classes matters far more than which securities you hold. Allocation dominates selection; selection is close to noise.
The big split, made once — decide stock vs. bond, confirm the stock side is globally diversified not home-biased, and write the target percentages down. Everything downstream implements this.
The one-fund test — the whole structure is usually one or two low-cost, global, all-in-one funds that rebalance themselves. If you're building something elaborate, that's the complexity error — check it.
The rebalance rule — set a band or a date and follow it mechanically. It sells high and buys low with no forecasting.
Diversification is the one move that cuts risk without cutting expected return. Canadians massively overweight Canada — ~3% of world markets — concentrating in one lopsided market for no return benefit.
4The Behaviour Gap
You are the largest risk to your own returns. The portfolio is rarely the problem; the hand on the portfolio is.
Write the IPS — the one-page policy is the master move (see the Output Layer). Pre-decide in calm what you'd otherwise decide badly in the moment.
Automate everything — contributions on payday into your allocation. You never feel like buying; you've already bought. The gap can't open where there's no discretionary hand.
The 24-hour rule — no trade, buy, sell or "adjustment" executes the day you first want it. Your mushin and "not relevant" cues are exactly this pillar's training, pointed at a screen.
The average dollar earned ~1.2%/yr less than its own funds over 2015–24 — self-inflicted, and eliminable for free by not acting. Losses hurt ~2× as much as gains feel good; a crash feels like an emergency when the right move is almost always nothing.
5The Tested Tilt
A systematic edge can be real — but the qualifications are the whole point. This is exactly where a smart, systems-minded builder blows himself up.
The five-point gate — out-of-sample · net of ALL costs · beats the index (not cash) risk-adjusted · statistically real (t>3 spirit) · survivable in drawdown. Pass all five or it's paper-trading, not capital.
Core-and-satellite cap — anything active or systematic capped hard, ~5–15%, with the boring index core for the rest. Decided in advance, not renegotiated when the satellite is hot.
The drought pre-commitment — write, before you buy: "this underperforms for years at a stretch; I hold, decided here in calm." Then execute the pre-decision in the drawdown.
Roughly half of a published anomaly's excess return tends to vanish after publication. A few factors are real and durable, but smaller than the back-test promises — and the ones you can access are the most crowded. Bring ARCHITECTURE.md for the five-point audit as its own session.
6Risk, Sizing & Ruin
Volatility isn't the enemy — permanent loss and running out of runway are. Size so no single outcome can end the game.
Bucket by horizon — money needed within ~2–3 years in safe, liquid assets; long-horizon capital in the market. Investing money you'll need soon is how a temporary 30% drop becomes a permanent loss.
Emergency fund first — several months of expenses in cash before any market pillar. It's what lets you hold through a crash without a forced sale, and stay non-reactive.
Fractional-Kelly + the ruin check — before any active bet ask "what if this goes maximally wrong?" If the honest answer forces a bad sale or sets back the exit, it's too big. Applies to the betting-intel system identically.
Lose 50% and you need +100% to recover; lose 100% and no edge saves you. Sequence-of-returns risk is THE thing to get right as the exit nears — a crash in the first drawdown years does far more damage. Its own session closer to the exit.
◎The Output Layer
The one-page Investment Policy Statement — the six pillars written down and committed. The highest-leverage artifact here.
What it states — the mission (the exit runway, family thriving), the target allocation, the accounts and their order, the contribution and rebalance rules, the behaviour hard lines, the satellite cap, the review cadence.
The hard lines — "When the market falls, I do not sell. No trade the day I first want it. I don't act on tips or media. I check only at my scheduled review." These outrank the in-the-moment feeling every time.
Write it, sign it, date it — then don't rewrite it under pressure, only in deliberate annual review. A market drop is the event the policy already anticipated, not new information.
This is your Orientation value hierarchy applied to capital — the deliberation done here, so that when the market arrives you execute instead of deliberate.
The inversion
The field's biggest failure mode is capable people optimising the one thing that barely matters — which securities to hold — while ignoring what actually compounds: cost, diversification, behaviour, time, and allocation. Optimise structure, not selection. Simple is not the beginner version you graduate from — simple is the destination.
The cultivation sequence
Weeks 1–2 — the structure. Open and prioritise the right accounts (RESP grant first if it applies), do the total-cost audit. Cheapest, highest-certainty change; nothing to predict.
Weeks 3–4 — allocation & automation. Make the one big split, implement it in one or two low-cost global funds, set automatic contributions. The core machine now runs itself.
Weeks 5–6 — the policy. Write the one-page IPS. Pillars 1 and 4 become rules, not intentions. Read them when the market is calm so they're loaded when it isn't.
Weeks 7+ — internalise, then leave alone. Most of the value is realised here, by the things you don't do.
Later, optional — the tilt. Only if the core is solid, only through the five-point gate, only as a capped satellite. No penalty for skipping it entirely.
The one thing
Get the money invested low-cost, broadly diversified, and global, inside the right accounts; automate the contributions; write one page of rules you commit to not overriding. That beats almost everything the prospector instinct pulls you toward — without needing you to be right about anything. The corollary, held just as firmly: don't run the scoring system with real capital until it has passed all five tests. Verify first. Real edges survive the wait.

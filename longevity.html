The Longevity Protocol

Longevity isn't the exotic stuff. At 47 the gains sit in a handful of boring, high-certainty levers — what you've already quit, sleep, aerobic fitness, muscle, and food. Optimise those; treat sauna, cold and fasting as garnish, not medicine.

1Know Your NumbersDo this first
One doctor's visit and a set of baseline labs outweigh any training tweak on this page — they turn a generic protocol into a targeted one.
CAC score — the single highest-value test for your age. A quick CT that catches heart disease years before symptoms; a score of zero is one of the most reassuring results in medicine. If you do one thing on this list, do this.
ApoB + full lipid panel — a better cardiovascular predictor than standard cholesterol. Ask for it by name.
Fasting glucose, A1c, blood pressure — metabolic and pressure baselines. BP matters here because cold and breath-holds spike it — know your starting point.
Liver enzymes (ALT/AST/GGT) — worth a look given the drinking history. The direct lever on fatty-liver risk is weight loss, not a supplement.
Vitamin D — very likely low at Vancouver's latitude in winter. Test, then correct a deficiency — don't blindly megadose.
VO₂max + grip strength — two of the strongest single mortality predictors that exist. Get a baseline so the training below has a number to move.
Strong signal: a 2025 study of 40,000+ patients found any coronary calcium at all meant 2–3× higher all-cause mortality over five years — and a zero holds reassuring out to two decades.
2The Two LeversBiggest wins
Moving from excess to rare is the biggest longevity win on this entire page. Nothing in training or supplements comes close.
Smoking to zero — not symmetric with drinking. Cardiovascular damage isn't linear; the first cigarette does a disproportionate share of the endothelial harm, so “just occasionally” is less safe than it feels. Quitting roughly halves cardiovascular risk inside a year. It's the highest-leverage thing left — and you're one step from done.
Drinking at monthly — sits at the very bottom of the risk curve. Not zero, but small enough that white-knuckling total abstinence could cost you more in stress than the alcohol does. That's a values call, not a medical emergency.
You already won the big fight. Don't let any protocol make you feel a rare indulgence is sabotaging you — at this frequency it isn't. From here the gains come from the boring pillars below.
3SleepMaybe the biggest
A 2025 analysis put insufficient sleep (under 7h) ahead of diet, activity or social ties as a predictor of shortened lifespan — and under six hours tracks to roughly double the cardiovascular mortality risk.
Fixed wake — 7:00 AM weekdays, within ±30 min on weekends. The single most important behavioural variable; sleeping in creates “social jet lag” that fragments the following week.
Room 18–20°C — the highest-ROI environmental lever. Your core has to drop ~1°C to initiate sleep; above 24°C fragments deep and REM sleep. Set the thermostat down by ~21:00.
Morning daylight, 5–10 min — even cloudy Vancouver light is 5,000–10,000+ lux, far past any indoor bulb. Anchors that night's melatonin, and matters more for you because the shifts disrupt it.
Caffeine cutoff 14:00 — half-life is 5–7 hours, so this clears enough before a ~10 PM lights-out. Caffeine steals deep sleep even when you fall asleep on time.
Dim, then dark — screens and lights down after ~21:00, blackout the room. Even a dimly lit room raises heart rate and impairs next-morning glucose versus true darkness.
Guard the Tue/Thu window — the shift nights compress you toward ~7 hours. That's the week's weak point: protect it fiercely, keep those days easy, and get daylight the next morning.
Optional add-ons: a warm shower 60–90 min before bed (vasodilation accelerates the core-temp drop); magnesium glycinate 200–400mg — not a melatonin replacement. Track on the Amazfit: sleep efficiency >85%, deep sleep >15%, judged on 7–14 day trends, never single nights.
4Aerobic Base — VO₂maxStrong
Cardiorespiratory fitness may matter more than your weight. A 2024 umbrella review of 20.9 million people put high-vs-low fitness at roughly half the all-cause mortality risk — a bigger effect than smoking, hypertension or obesity.
Zone 2 is the quiet winner — the long Sunday session builds the aerobic base that lifts VO₂max over time. Each 1-MET gain tracks to ~11–17% lower mortality, at every rung of the ladder. Bias volume here; it's low-cost, high-return.
Fit beats lean — a 398,000-person analysis found fit-but-overweight people had no significant mortality bump over fit-and-lean, while unfit people ran 2–3× the risk regardless of weight. At 200 lb, raising fitness beats chasing the scale.
Let HIIT carry intensity — twice a week (Wed/Sat), off your shift days. Since Zone 2 is now only once a week, make the intervals genuinely hard so they hold up the aerobic side.
5Strength & PowerStrong
Muscle is late-life independence. Grip strength is one of the cleanest single mortality predictors we have — each 5 kg drop is about 16% higher all-cause risk in a 140,000-person study.
Compound lifts, 3×/week — Mon (heavier / lower), Tue (moderate upper, ~45 min on the shift day), Fri (compound + grip). Keep the three distinct so back-to-backs recover.
Train for speed, not just load — moving a weight fast (power) predicts mortality even better than raw strength. Make some lifting phases explosive. Cheap upgrade, big signal.
Grip work — loaded carries, hangs, hard pulls. It's a proxy for whole-body robustness, not just forearms.
Night-shift caution: shift work with circadian disruption is an IARC probable carcinogen. You may not have a choice about Tue/Thu — so keep the Tuesday lift moderate, don't stack intensity on a compressed day, and protect the sleep you can get.
6DietStrong
For body composition and longevity, diet is the bigger lever — and getting leaner does more for your liver, lipids and glucose than any pill.
Protein ~145 g/day — roughly 1.6 g/kg, spread across meals at ~30–40 g each (synthesis caps per sitting). The single most protective diet change for holding onto muscle as you age.
Mediterranean base — plants, legumes, olive oil, fish, nuts, whole grains; little ultra-processed food. Not magic, just the pattern that shows up repeatedly in the strongest trial data (PREDIMED).
Fibre 30 g+/day from food — most people get about half that, and it's one of the cleaner long-term predictors. Cheap, easy, ignored.
Post-meal walk, 10–15 min — meaningfully blunts the glucose spike. Even a lap of the building counts. (This is the piece of “break up sitting” worth keeping — acute glucose is a real, separate endpoint from the mortality one your training volume already covers.)
Liver-history note: watch fatty-liver risk and go easy on fructose-heavy processed food. Weight down = liver, lipids and glucose all improve together.
◎The GarnishReal, not pillars
Separated from the decorative “aligns with mitochondrial function” language, which sounds like a mechanism and means nothing you can act on. Keep these — just don't score them as core levers.
Supplements — creatine 3–5 g/day (genuinely worth it: muscle, strength, maybe cognition; timing irrelevant), omega-3/DHA (keep), Vitamin D if low, magnesium glycinate for sleep. Skip NMN, resveratrol, taurine, spermidine — human evidence runs thin to absent. Your money buys more life as a CT scan than a cabinet of pills.
Sauna — the best-supported extra (Finnish cohorts show a dose-response with lower mortality), but observational and confounded. A modest bonus; keep it 4+ hours from bed.
Cold exposure — oversold. Longevity claims are extrapolated from mood and metabolic studies, no solid human lifespan evidence, and it spikes blood pressure. Keep only if you enjoy it; don't score it as a lever.
Sunday fast — skip the autophagy hype; the human longevity case is thin. The real benefit is it nudges weight down — keep it if it helps you eat less overall.
Breath-holds / CO₂ work — improve tolerance and calm, not proven to extend life. Given the smoking history, ordinary cardio does far more for actual lung function.
One hard safety line: never do breath-holds or hyperventilation protocols in or near water, or while driving. Shallow-water blackout kills experienced people every year. This one isn't a formality.
The training week
Mon — Strength. Heavier / lower body, loaded carries. Full evening.
Tue Night shift — Strength. Moderate upper body, ~45 min in the pre-5:30 window. The lightest of the three, so Wednesday's legs stay fresh.
Wed — HIIT. Hard intervals, ~20 min.
Thu Night shift — Rest. Protect sleep. Slow-breathing (5–6/min, 5 min) before you leave; nothing hard.
Fri — Strength. Compound lifts + grip.
Sat — HIIT. Second hard day.
Sun — Long Zone 2, 45–90 min. The aerobic base. Optional 8–12h light fasting window.
Priority order
Keep smoking near zero → protect sleep (7+ hrs) → know your numbers (CAC + labs) → build VO₂max and strength → hit protein and eat Mediterranean. Everything else is a bonus stacked on top. Sauna, cold and fasting are the garnish — real, but garnish.
The one thing
If you do nothing else new: protect the 7-hour sleep window on your Tue/Thu shift nights, and keep smoking at zero. Those two outrank every supplement and every training tweak on this page.
For the doctor, not the shelf
Two items that are physician decisions, flagged only so they're on your radar: the CAC score in Pillar 1, and GLP-1 medications (semaglutide and similar) — 2024–25 research increasingly treats them as more than weight-loss drugs, with a large trial showing a ~20% drop in major cardiovascular events in overweight people without diabetes. Real trade-offs and cost; a conversation to have, not something to self-prescribe.
Not medical advice. Book the doctor's visit in Pillar 1 and let a physician own the labs and anything you take daily, especially alongside the liver history.

The Return

Twelve weeks from eighteen months off to the training week in the Longevity Protocol. A bridge, not a destination — that page already owns labs, sleep, Zone 2 vs HIIT, protein and the garnish tier, and none of it is restated here.
Tap to add an image
Change
Why this exists. The Longevity Protocol's week — Mon/Tue/Fri lifts, Wed/Sat HIIT, Sunday long Zone 2 — is correct for a trained body. Started cold on a grade III ankle and a bad elbow after eighteen months out, it is the fastest route back to being injured. This gets you to the point where that week is the right week, and then hands you over to it.
The one principle
Tissue lags capacity
Muscle adapts in weeks. Tendon, ligament and the neural map of a joint adapt in months.
Your strength will come back far faster than the tissue that has to tolerate it. This is why returning lifters get hurt in weeks four to eight — not week one, when they are careful, but week six, when the muscle feels ready and the connective tissue is six weeks behind. B
You advance on gates, not on dates. If a phase's gate is not met, you repeat the week. Twelve weeks is the fast version, not the schedule.
The two constraints
01
The ankle
Four torn ligaments is grade III. It governs the whole programme.
Up to 70% of lateral ankle sprains leave chronic ankle instability behind — lost dorsiflexion range, degraded single-leg balance, weak peroneals, and altered neuromuscular control measured at spinal and cortical level, not just locally. A
The sense that the left leg is longer, when it measures the same, is that picture exactly: the brain's positional map of the foot has not fully updated, so the limb feels wrong because the signal is degraded. A for the deficits, D for reading your particular sensation that way.
A stiff, poorly controlled ankle changes squat mechanics and running gait, and pushes the load it cannot absorb up into knee, hip and low back.
It does not fix itself through general conditioning. In one trial the control group that simply cycled improved on nothing. B
02
The elbow
Cane use loads the wrist extensors through grip, so this is almost certainly lateral.
First-line treatment is progressive tendon loading — not rest, and not isometrics specifically. The 2015 isometric-analgesia finding did not replicate, and meta-analysis found isometric no better than isotonic for chronic tendinopathy. B
It gates pulling. Rows, deadlifts, carries and chin-ups are all grip-limited, so they come in light, with straps and neutral grips where those help.
Week 1 — baselines
Do these before you train anything. Fifteen minutes, repeatable, and without them you have no idea whether the ankle work is doing anything. Log them in the Work tab and re-test at weeks 4, 8 and 12.
1
Knee-to-wall
Dorsiflexion range, in centimetres.
Foot square to a wall, drive the knee forward to touch without the heel lifting. Slide the foot back to the furthest point where the knee still touches and the heel stays down. Measure big toe to wall. Both sides.
Target: left within 1.5 cm of right. C
2
Single-leg balance
Postural control, in seconds.
Hands on hips, other foot off the ground. Time to the first touch-down or hop. Eyes open, then eyes closed. Both sides.
Target: 45 s eyes open, 30 s eyes closed, left matching right. C
3
Single-leg heel raise
Calf endurance, in reps.
Full height, controlled, one leg, fingertips on a wall for balance only. Count good reps to failure. Both sides.
Target: 25 reps, left within 3 of right. C
4
Elbow load
Tendon tolerance, in pounds.
Grip a dumbbell, wrist neutral, lower slowly into wrist flexion. Note the weight at which pain reaches 4/10 — and whether it has settled by the next morning.
The next-morning answer matters more than the number. C
Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4
Rebuild the joint, build the engine
Ankle range, balance and peroneal strength; elbow loading tolerance; aerobic base with zero impact. No barbell. No running.
Daily — ankle, 10 minutes, every day
Knee-to-wall mobilisation — 3 × 30 s each side, heel down, driving into the stretch.
Single-leg balance — 4 × 30 s left, 2 × 30 s right. Weeks 1–2 eyes open on the floor; weeks 3–4 eyes closed, or eyes open on a folded towel. One variable at a time. B
Band eversion — 3 × 15 each side, slow. The peroneal work, and the most neglected piece of ankle rehab.
Calf raises — bilateral, 3 × 15, straight-knee. Add bent-knee (soleus) sets from week 3.
Most days — elbow, 5 minutes
Eccentric wrist extension — light dumbbell, raise with the other hand, lower over 3–4 seconds. 3 × 15. Pain to 4/10 during is expected; it must be settled by morning. If it is not, drop the weight. B
Lifting — 2 sessions a week, full body, ~40 min
Machines and supported positions only. No free-weight squat, no deadlift, no overhead press from the floor. Two to three sets, 8–12 reps, stopping with four reps still in the tank. You are not making progress yet — you are reminding tissue what load is.

Cardio — 3 sessions, 30–40 min
Bike or elliptical, Zone 2 — conversational, you could hold a sentence. No running. No rowing: the rower demands exactly the dorsiflexion you do not have yet.
Gate to Phase 2. Knee-to-wall left within 2 cm of right · balance 30 s eyes open on the left with no touch-down · elbow under 4/10 on the loading set and settled by morning · three cardio sessions in each of weeks 3 and 4.
Phase 2 · Weeks 5–8
Load it
Real strength training. Free weights return. Still no impact.
Ankle work continues — progressed
Balance moves to single-leg with head turns, then single-leg reaches in three directions (forward, diagonal back-left, diagonal back-right), 5 each way, 3 rounds.
Calf raises go single-leg, 3 × 12, straight and bent-knee. Band eversion stays.
Lifting — 3 sessions a week, full body, ~50 min
Three to four sets, 6–10 reps, now stopping with two reps in reserve. Add load when you hit the top of the rep range on every set. Introduce one new movement per week, in this order:
5
Goblet squat — only to the depth your dorsiflexion allows without the left heel lifting. Heels on a 1 cm plate is a legitimate fix, not a cheat.
6
Romanian deadlift — dumbbells, straps on, light.
7
Split squat — rear foot on the floor, not elevated. The first real loaded single-leg test of the ankle. Watch for the knee drifting inward.
8
Overhead press — dumbbells, neutral grip.
Cardio — 4 sessions
Three Zone 2 of 40–50 min, plus one interval session on the bike: four minutes hard, three easy, four rounds. The best-evidenced protocol for moving VO₂max, run at full intensity with zero ankle load. A
Gate to Phase 3. Single-leg heel raise 20 reps on the left · balance 30 s eyes closed on the left · loaded split squat pain-free with the knee tracking straight · elbow tolerating a straps-off set with no next-morning soreness.
Phase 3 · Weeks 9–12
Intensity and impact
Running and power come back. This is the phase where the ankle finds out.
Impact progression — the slow part
9
Hops in place — double-leg 3 × 10, then single-leg left 3 × 5, landing softly and holding a second.
10
Walk/jog — 1 min jog, 2 min walk, 10 rounds. Flat, even ground only.
11
Walk/jog — 2 min jog, 2 min walk, 8 rounds.
12
Continuous — 20 minutes easy, flat.
Never on trails, gravel or camber. Uneven ground is how grade III ankles become grade III ankles again. If a session leaves the ankle sore next morning, repeat the previous week — no exceptions, nothing made up later.
The VO₂max number arrives here
The Amazfit Active Max calculates VO₂max only in Outdoor Running, Trail Running and Track Run modes — Amazfit's own spec, and why the field has always read “no data.” It stays blank until roughly week 10. Chasing it earlier means running before the ankle is ready. A
Lifting — 3 sessions a week
Three to five sets, 5–8 reps, one to two in reserve. Add speed work: one exercise per session moved fast on the concentric. Power predicts mortality better than raw strength. B
Add loaded carries — farmer's walks, bare grip, light. The elbow is the gate: start with a weight you could carry twice as far as you do.
Cardio — 4 to 5 sessions
Two Zone 2 at 45–60 min, one bike 4×4, plus the run progression. Total lands near 150–180 min a week, which is where the Longevity Protocol wants you.
Gate to handoff. Knee-to-wall left within 1.5 cm · balance 45 s eyes open and 30 s eyes closed on the left · heel raise 25 reps, left within 3 of right · 20 minutes continuous running with no next-day ankle soreness · elbow a non-issue on all pulling.
Week 13 — hand off
This protocol ends
Open the Longevity Protocol and run its training week as written. That page is the programme from here.
Keep two things permanently: the single-leg balance work (five minutes — it is what stops the next sprain) and the knee-to-wall measurement monthly.
Rules that override everything above
The 24-hour rule. Soreness during a session is fine. Soreness next morning in a joint — not a muscle — means the last session was too much. Repeat, do not advance.
Never advance two variables at once. New exercise or more load, not both. New surface or eyes closed, not both.
One deload week. Week 8 or wherever the fatigue lands: half the sets, same movements. Eighteen months off means recovery capacity is detrained too.
Tuesdays and Thursdays are not training days. Not home until after 21:30 and the sleep window is already the weak point.
When a week is busy, cut lifting before the ankle work. It is ten minutes and it protects everything else.
What I would spend money on. One or two physiotherapy sessions, early — not for a programme, for a measurement. A physio can assess that ankle's dorsiflexion, balance and peroneal strength properly in twenty minutes and say whether these are the right gates for your ankle. Everything here is reasoned from the literature on grade III sprains in general, not from your ankle, because it cannot be seen from here.
The one thing
You advance on gates, not on dates. The calendar is an estimate. The numbers in the Work tab are not.
Evidence
A — strong trial or meta-analytic support.
B — good support, some inconsistency.
C — clinically standard figure, not trial-derived.
D — reasoned judgement.

The physiology here is well-supported. The specific numbers are not: the gate thresholds are clinical conventions C, and the twelve-week shape is a judgement about how fast a detrained 47-year-old with these two constraints can reasonably move D. Treat the gates as real and the calendar as an estimate.
Not medical advice. Clearance to exercise is not the same as clearance for a specific return-to-running progression, which is a physio's call.

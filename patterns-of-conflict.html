Patterns of Conflict

After Col. John Boyd, Patterns of Conflict (1987 version, A Discourse on Winning and Losing). The sibling of Shaping and Adapting: that one is about keeping your own picture true, this one is about acting against something that pushes back.

The idea
The aim is not to win the fightDOCTRINE
Boyd opens the briefing with organisms, not armies. The aim of anything alive is to survive, to survive on its own terms, and to improve its capacity for independent action. Where resources are contested, that may require diminishing someone else’s capacity for the same — but the diminishing is downstream of the aim, never the aim itself.
Everything below follows from that, and so does the main practical consequence: destroying the opposing force is almost never the objective. If you find yourself fighting something without being able to say what capacity you gain by winning, you are in a fight that has replaced its own purpose.
Four qualities recur in every case Boyd found worth studying: variety of response, rapidity in applying it, harmony so your own parts don’t work against each other, and initiative, because you cannot shape change by reacting to it.
Three levels, and the shallowest is the loud oneDOCTRINE
Boyd sorts conflict into three levels. Physical — resources, position, force. Mental — the opponent’s picture of what is happening. Moral — trust, will, and whether his own people and commitments hold together.
Most conflicts are fought entirely on the bottom band.
The physical level is the most visible and the least decisive. The moral level is the reverse. This is the single most portable idea in the briefing, and it is why the moves below are mostly not about force.
Where this applies, and where it must not. This is adversarial doctrine. It belongs in domains with a real counterparty whose interests genuinely oppose yours — income and negotiation, the stock and betting systems, a dispute where someone is actually working against you. It does not belong with your daughters, your students, your colleagues or your own family. Treating a cooperative relationship as a conflict to be won corrodes it, and the corrosion is quiet and slow. Your own orientation protocol already scopes strategy and cunning to competitive domains for exactly this reason. Two moves below — Tempo, not speed and Intent, not instructions — are about your own side’s harmony rather than an opponent, and those carry into the classroom and the house safely. The rest do not.
The seven moves
Name the opposing forceC
Before anything else, say out loud what is actually opposing you — and whether it is an adversary or just a condition.
Use when something feels like a fight: a stalled project, a market that keeps taking your money, a negotiation, a situation you keep describing in combat language.
Name it in one sentence. Who or what is it, and what does it want that is incompatible with what you want?
Sort it: adversary or condition. An adversary adapts to you. A condition doesn’t — it has no plan, no morale and nothing to isolate. Entropy is a condition. A counterparty is an adversary.
If it is a condition, stop here and leave. None of the other six moves work on it; the Entropy protocol does. Most of what feels like an enemy turns out to be drift, cost or your own attention.
Say what winning buys you. Which capacity do you gain? If you cannot answer, you are fighting for the sake of fighting, and the fight is itself the displacement.
In practice
IncomeThe obstacle to the exit number is almost never a competitor.It is available hours and unfinished work — conditions. Which means the answer is scheduling and finishing, not strategy.
Stock systemThe market is a genuine counterparty, but it is not opposing you personally.It adapts in aggregate, which is why an edge decays. That makes it adversary-like in one respect only: whatever you found, others are finding.
NegotiationA contract, a rate, a purchase — a real adversary with a plan and a fallback.This is where the rest of the page earns its keep.
Failure signal: you are using conflict language about something that cannot fight back. If your opponent has no plan you could attack, you have misnamed it.
Attack the plan, not the forceDOCTRINE
Boyd takes Sun Tzu’s ordering intact: go after his plans first, his alliances second, his forces third, and his strongholds only when there is no alternative.
Use when you have named a real adversary and your instinct is to go straight at their strength.
Find the assumption the plan rests on. Every plan has one thing that must be true. That is the target, and it is usually cheaper to reach than the force itself.
Work down the list in order. Plans, then the relationships that support them, then the force. Attacking the strong point first is the most expensive way to win and the most common instinct.
Subduing without fighting is the highest form. If the situation resolves because the other side’s plan no longer works, you have spent nothing.
In practice
NegotiationAsk what their plan assumes about you — that you’re in a hurry, that you have no alternative, that you’ll split the difference.Arranging one real alternative before you talk does more than any argument made at the table.
IncomeWhen a channel is crowded, the competitors’ shared assumption is the opening.If every course in the space assumes buyers want theory, a method that produces evidence in eight weeks attacks the assumption rather than the sellers.
Stock systemAn edge is an assumption others hold that is wrong.Write down what the crowd must believe for your setup to pay. If you can’t state it, you don’t have an edge, you have a pattern.
Failure signal: your plan is to work harder at the thing they are strongest at. That is the storming-the-city option, chosen first.
Hold with one hand, decide with the otherDOCTRINE
Boyd’s reading of Sun Tzu’s cheng and ch’i: an orthodox effort that fixes attention, and an unorthodox one that decides the thing. Which means going deliberately thin somewhere.
Use when you have limited hours and several fronts, and you are currently giving all of them equal attention.
Split the effort in two. What merely has to hold, and what actually has to win. They are rarely the same thing, and they should never get the same resources.
Choose where to be weak, explicitly. Epaminondas’s contribution at Leuctra was not concentrating force — everyone knew that — it was deliberately thinning part of the line to do it. Weakness you chose is a decision; weakness you drifted into is a mistake.
Name the decisive point, once, in writing. If everything is decisive, nothing is getting concentrated effort.
In practice
IncomeTwo spare hours a week is a single point of concentration, not a portfolio.The course is the decisive effort; the books and the systems hold. Holding means maintained and not abandoned — it does not mean advanced.
Room 148Decide which course gets the real build this term and which gets good-enough reuse.Reusing last year’s material somewhere is the thin line that pays for a genuinely new unit elsewhere. Spreading the build evenly produces four mediocre ones.
SummerThe break is the real capacity, so the thinning decision is made in June, not August.Name the one thing the summer is for before it starts, and let the rest hold.
Failure signal: you cannot name anywhere you are deliberately weak. Then you are thin everywhere and concentrated nowhere.
Many probes, reinforce what movesDOCTRINE
Several cheap thrusts, then commit behind whichever one finds give. Not one big plan executed faithfully.
Use when the ground is genuinely uncertain and you are tempted to pick the single best option and commit to it in advance.
Send several cheap probes rather than one considered bet. The probe’s job is to produce information you cannot get by thinking.
Reinforce success, not the plan. The hard part is withdrawing from the effort you liked most. A probe that fails has done its job — it cost little and it told you something.
Keep them ambiguous while they run. Several live options mean no single counter works against you; it also means you have not committed before you had to.
In practice
IncomeThree small offers beat one polished launch.Put the method in front of three different audiences cheaply, then build the finished thing behind whichever one replies.
Stock systemThis is what the paper and shadow tracks already are.The discipline is the second half: size up behind what the forward test rewards, and cut what it doesn’t, including the variant you find most interesting.
Room 148Try two versions of an activity across two blocks rather than perfecting one.The block that goes better is data you could not have got from planning.
Failure signal: every probe gets reinforced, or the one you hoped for gets reinforced regardless of what it returned. Then they were not probes, they were commitments in disguise.
Tempo, not speedB
Close the cycle before the situation changes. That is a different instruction from going faster, and the pop version of the OODA loop gets it wrong.
Use when a decision is in front of you and you are still gathering. Safe in every domain, including home and the classroom — it is about your own cycle, not an opponent.
Set the closing condition before you start. “I decide by Friday” or “I decide once I have X.” A cycle with no closing condition reopens forever, which is the mechanism behind never feeling settled. A Implementation intentions — a written when–then — roughly double follow-through.
Match the tempo to the ground, not to your nerves. Fast where the situation is moving, slow where it isn’t. Speed applied to a stable situation is just churn.
Let the act finish the picture. Reality supplies the last data only after you move. Orientation does not complete first and then release you.
Route it through the three-tier rule you already have. Three seconds if nothing survives the night; seven breaths if it is consequential and already in front of you; Sunday if it opens a new loop.
In practice
IncomeShip the version that is good enough to get a reply.The reply is the information. Another week of polish produces none of it.
Room 148A unit decision made Sunday and tested Monday beats a better one made Thursday.You get four days of feedback instead of nought.
HomeSafe here, and the version that matters: decide and be present, rather than deciding all evening.Which room, what to say, put the phone down — three seconds, no deliberation. Auditing these is the problem.
Failure signal: your picture keeps improving and nothing gets committed. Tempo has become polish.
Cut his connections, multiply your ownDOCTRINE
Boyd’s compression of the entire briefing: isolate the adversary morally, mentally and physically, while connecting yourself to more centres of power than he has.
Use when you are in a genuine contest and want the highest-leverage thing available. Competitive domains only.
Count his connections and yours. Who supplies him, backs him, refers him, tells him the truth? Then answer the same for yourself. The count is usually the whole diagnosis.
Build your side first. The multiplying half is available immediately, is not adversarial and carries no cost if you are wrong about the fight.
Isolation means cutting support, not people. Winning an alliance away, becoming the better option, removing his information advantage. If a tactic requires deceiving or damaging someone, it has left this protocol — the Credible Floor rule applies: capacity, not permission.
In practice
IncomeThe multiplying half is nearly the whole opportunity here.One channel and no audience is an isolated position regardless of how good the product is. Every new connection raises the floor.
NegotiationThe side with more live alternatives wins without arguing.Two other quotes in hand is isolation and interaction in one move.
Block BNon-adversarial reading, and the one that applies: three host teachers is three connections to build.A support role with no relationship to the host teacher is an isolated position, and the students pay for it.
Failure signal: you are working on the cutting half and have done nothing about the multiplying half. That ordering is a tell — it is the more satisfying half and the less effective one.
Intent, not instructionsB
Boyd’s reading of the German pattern: name the point of main effort, tell people what the intent is, and let them work out how. Tempo cannot be run through a central node.
Use when anything involves other people acting without you in the room. Safe in every domain.
Give the intent and the boundaries, not the steps. What outcome, by when, and what must not happen. Then leave the method alone. B Autonomy over method is one of the better-supported predictors of both performance and engagement.
Name the main effort so people can judge for themselves. Someone who knows what matters most can make a good call you never anticipated. Someone following steps cannot.
Trust is the enabler, not a virtue. Shared understanding is what makes dispersed action coherent, and it is built by being predictable and honest before it is needed. B
In practice
Room 148Give the criteria and the outcome rather than the procedure.“A reader should be able to picture the place” travels further than eight steps, and it survives contact with a student who takes a different route.
FLA 6/7Adults especially: say what the work is for.Intent makes the presentation schedule something they manage themselves rather than something you chase.
HomeThe domestic version — say what the evening is for, not how each person does their part.Harmony here means the parts aren’t working against each other, which is most of what a household argument actually is.
Failure signal: everything routes back through you for a decision. That is not a delegation problem; it means the intent was never stated, only the steps.

The Sunday block
Four questionsweekly · 5 min
What did I treat as an adversary this week that was actually a condition?
Where was I deliberately weak — and was it a decision or a drift?
Which cycle stayed open all week, and what closing condition would have shut it?
Did I add a connection, or only defend a position?
Not wired into the debrief. Read them in the existing Sunday review.
The one thing
The aim is not to win the fight. It is to improve my capacity for independent action. If winning doesn’t buy that, the fight is the displacement.
Failure modes
Opponent inflation. Once you have conflict vocabulary, everything starts looking like an adversary. Move one exists to stop this and should be doing most of the work.
Importing it into the wrong rooms. Strategy applied to your daughters or your students reads as manipulation whether or not it is meant that way, and it is corrosive slowly enough that you won’t notice for months.
Cutting before building. The isolation half is more satisfying than the connection half, and much less useful.
Tempo as a licence for speed. Moving fast on new projects to escape important ones is your documented failure mode; the three-tier rule is the guard, not this page.
Reading the briefing as being about war. Boyd’s own aim was organisms surviving on their own terms. If the takeaway feels aggressive, the reading has gone wrong somewhere.
Evidence
DOCTRINE — Boyd’s own synthesis of military history and his reading of Sun Tzu, Epaminondas, the Mongols and the German pattern. Coherent, influential and widely taught, but it is interpretation, not an empirical finding. No study sits behind it and none is claimed.
A — strong, replicated experimental support. Used here only for implementation intentions (Gollwitzer).
B — good supporting research, smaller or narrower: autonomy over method, shared understanding and trust in teams, recognition-primed decision making under time pressure (Klein).
C — reasoned from your own records and this system’s history rather than from research.
D — weak or contested. Nothing on this page is graded D; if something later earns it, it says so.
Boyd never published. The briefing was delivered aloud over about fifteen hours and revised nineteen times; the slides are terse because he talked over them. Grant Hammond compiled the August 1987 version for Air University Press. The OODA diagram everyone associates with him is not in it — that came later, and Shaping and Adapting covers it.

Size and Strength

Twelve weeks, four lifting days, size first with strength built in, losing fat at the same time. It starts where The Return hands off, and for these twelve weeks it replaces the training week in the Longevity Protocol. Log every set in the Lift Log.

How the plan works

Four rules that run everything
Effort. Most growth comes from sets taken close to failure. RIR means reps in reserve: RIR 2 is stopping with two good reps left. B
Double progression. When you hit the top of the rep range on every set, add the smallest weight jump next session and drop back to the bottom of the range. D
Rest. 2–3 minutes after heavy main lifts, at least 90 seconds after everything else. Under 60 seconds costs growth; past 90 seconds makes little difference. B
Pairs. A1/A2 alternate: a set of A1, rest, a set of A2, rest. Leg and upper-body work don't tire each other, so you fit more good sets into the hour. D
Split versus full body makes no difference once weekly volume is equal, so the pairing is chosen for your ankle and the clock, not because it beats anything. A
The twelve weeks
Ramp three weeks, build four, deload, build three, retest. Tendons adapt more slowly than muscle, which is why the ramp exists.

Week 12 retest. Bench, seated overhead press, heavy leg press and lat pulldown: warm up, then one all-out set of clean reps at your week-11 weight. More reps than week 4 at the same or higher weight means you got stronger. No true one-rep max tests. D
Muscle memory. Muscle built before keeps extra nuclei after you stop, so early progress should come faster than for a true beginner. C

Weekly hard sets, build weeks
Chest 13 · back 13 plus face pulls · quads 10 · hamstrings 12 plus hip thrusts · side shoulders 9 plus pressing · biceps 6 and triceps 6, plus all the pulling and pressing. Compound lifts give arms and shoulders real indirect work. B
Stretch-position picks. Deep flyes, incline curls, overhead extensions and Romanian deadlifts are deliberate: training a muscle at long length looks like a real growth bonus. C
Swaps. Any exercise can become a machine or cable version that trains the same muscle through a similar range. Keep a swap at least four weeks so progress is measurable. The fly uses dumbbells because the gym has no crossover station and the pec deck can't reach a full stretch where it sits.
Ankle rules
Load the ankle gradually and keep it out of balance-heavy positions. Your physio overrides anything here.
Pain check. Ankle pain up to 3/10 during or after is acceptable if it has settled by next morning. Higher, or still sore next morning, means drop that exercise's load back one step for a week. B D
Leg press. Feet high on the platform means less ankle bend. Lower them a little at a time only while the ankle stays quiet. D
Seated calf raises. Weeks 1–3 no added weight, slow full range. From week 4, add the smallest plate only after two pain-free weeks at the current load. D
Out for the twelve weeks unless your physio clears them: lunges, split squats, step-ups, jumping, sprinting, heavy standing calf raises. D
Romanian deadlifts. You stand still, so the ankle carries little, but start light in case standing under load bothers it. D
Keep from The Return. Five minutes of single-leg balance most days and knee-to-wall monthly, as that page says to keep permanently.
Nutrition and supplement timing
155–190 g of protein a day in four or five servings of 35–40 g, calories at maintenance or slightly below, 5 g creatine daily. Extended fasts paused for the twelve weeks.
Amount. About 1.6 g/kg a day captures most of the benefit, roughly 155 g at your weight. A Older muscle needs a bigger dose per meal to respond fully, about 0.4 g/kg, or 35–40 g. B This replaces the Longevity Protocol's 145 g for the twelve weeks.
Calories. Building muscle while losing fat works at maintenance or a small deficit when protein is high. B Aim to lose 0.5–1 lb a week on the weekly average. Flat two weeks with no waist change: cut 200–300 calories a day. Dropping faster than 1 lb a week, or lifts stalled two weeks: add about 200. C D

Supplements
Creatine monohydrate, 3–5 g every day, any time; plain or micronized monohydrate, not HCl, buffered, ethyl ester or gummies. Start now rather than saving it for a plateau: its water-weight jump then lands inside the ramp, not in the middle of your fat-loss readings. Loading at 20 g a day for 5–7 days is optional. A
Whey, 35–40 g at breakfast, and after training if dinner is more than two hours away. Convenience, not magic. A
Casein, Greek yogurt or cottage cheese before bed, about 40 g protein. B
Vitamin D only if a blood test shows you're low; take it with a meal containing fat. D
Caffeine up to about 200 mg, 30–60 minutes before training; skip it if sleep suffers. A D
Look for NSF Certified for Sport, Informed Sport or Creapure; a Canadian NPN means Health Canada licensed the product, not that each batch was tested. Skip BCAAs, testosterone boosters and pre-workout blends beyond their caffeine. B
Tracking progress
Lifts going up, waist down or holding, bodyweight trending slowly down. The scale alone can't tell fat loss from muscle gain.

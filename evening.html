The Evening Protocol

Lights out 23:00, fixed. Two versions: a standard night, and the long Tuesday and Thursday when you get in after 21:30. The long one is subtraction, not a shorter version of the other.

The design, in one idea
Racing thoughts at lights-out are not solved in bed. They are relocated. In a randomised study with polysomnography, people who spent five minutes writing a specific to-do list for the next day fell asleep measurably faster than those who wrote about tasks already finished — and the more specific the list, the faster the onset. That is why the offload is the load-bearing step and everything else is support.
Standard night
Long night — Tuesday and Thursday
Fourteen hours of work and home after 21:30. The offload at 21:00 does not exist on these nights, so the whole shape changes. The goodnight comes first — it is the reason you come straight home, and the offload works better once the day is actually over. Nothing gets added on these nights except the Tuesday casein shake, which rides in the gap the goodnight already creates. The only target is reaching 23:00 without the day still running.
Rules that don't move
The wake time is the anchor, not the bedtime — 07:00 regardless of how the night went. It is the component people abandon first and the one the whole system hangs on.
Do not extend the sleep window to fix bad onset — compressing time in bed is one of the two active components of CBT-I and it works by raising sleep pressure. More time awake in bed means a longer latency the next night, not a shorter one.
The exit rule — awake and frustrated for around twenty minutes, get out of bed and do something dull in dim light until sleepy. This is stimulus control, and it is doing real work, not being tidy.
Warm extremities, cool room — socks on, room at 18–19°C. Peripheral warming drives heat away from the core, which is the direction you need.
Total darkness — even moderate light during sleep raised heart rate and impaired next-morning glucose metabolism in the Northwestern study. Curtains or a mask.
Alcohol is the trap — it is the most effective sleep-onset aid available and one of the worst sleep-quality interventions available. It shortens onset and then wrecks the second half of the night.
Where training sits
Mon, Tue, Wed, Fri, Sat. Thursday off.
Tuesday trains inside the working block, not after it — you are not home until 21:35, and a session that late would fight the temperature drop you are trying to produce.
The shower after training is warm, not cold — cold immediately after resistance work measurably blunts hypertrophy and strength adaptation. It also happens to be the version that helps you sleep.
The casein shake follows training, not the calendar — Mon, Wed, Fri and Sat at 21:20, Tuesday at 21:45. Thursday and Sunday get nothing, which is what keeps two uncompromised fasts in the week.
The honest part
Two fourteen-hour days a week is a larger variable in your health than anything in this document. A wind-down sequence does not offset it, and I am not going to write as though it does. What this protocol can do is stop those nights from also costing you the sleep.
The one thing
If everything else falls over: do the offload. Five specific lines about tomorrow, written down, away from the bed. It is the only step here with a randomised trial measuring the outcome you actually care about.
